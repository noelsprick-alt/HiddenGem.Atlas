My story in Hamburg

I was in Hamburg, Bremen, Lübeck and Rügen – and every corner tells of the time when Europe’s mightiest merchants sat here. My tip for Hamburg: the fish market very early on a Sunday, then the HADAG ferry from the Landungsbrücken – a harbour tour for the price of a bus ticket. Getting there and around

You reach Hamburg by ICE, flight or coach. In the city S-Bahn, U-Bahn and the HADAG ferries run on the local transit fare.

Hamburg is well connected: the main station is a hub for ICE, regional traffic and S-Bahn, and the airport can be reached by S-Bahn in about 25 minutes (check timetables beforehand). In the city you use the HVV with U-Bahn, S-Bahn, bus and ferries; a day ticket pays off.

The old town, Speicherstadt and HafenCity are easy to explore on foot. For St Pauli, Altona and Blankenese you take the train.

1. Must-sees

Fish market on Sunday morning

Very early on Sunday to the Altona fish market.

The Altona fish market takes place on Sunday in the early morning hours: traders call out their wares – fish, fruit, flowers, cheese, sweets – and the famous “Aale-Dieter” loudly praise eel and baskets full of goods. In the Fischauktionshalle there is live music and breakfast; after a night out, night owls and early risers meet here.

In summer the market starts around 5 a.m. and ends around 9:30 a.m., in winter it opens later (check times beforehand). Come early, bring cash and try a fish roll or fried fish.

Tip: Come before 7 a.m. and have breakfast in the Fischauktionshalle.

HADAG ferry from the Landungsbrücken

A harbour tour for the price of a bus ticket.

The HADAG ferries are part of Hamburg’s public transport and run on the Elbe – you pay the normal HVV fare and get a harbour tour for free. Line 62 leads from the Landungsbrücken past container terminals, shipyards, the fish market and Övelgönne to Finkenwerder. From the deck you see the Elbphilharmonie, harbour cranes and big container ships.

The ride is most beautiful in the late afternoon or at sunset. Bring a jacket – it is windy on the water. Check timetables and fares beforehand.

Tip: Sit on the outer deck and ride in the evening light.

Elbphilharmonie Plaza

In the evening the 360° view of harbour and city. 4.6 stars on Google. Visitors report that a free ticket is needed for the Plaza; check info beforehand.

The Elbphilharmonie opened in 2017 and stands on an old quay warehouse. The Plaza lies at a height of 37 metres and offers an outdoor promenade around the building with a view of harbour, city and Elbe. Access is free but tied to a ticket that you get online or on site (check current conditions beforehand).

A long curved escalator leads up. Come in the evening at sunset and stay until dark – then the harbour lights glow. Concerts in the Great Hall are in demand; if you want tickets you should plan well in advance.

🕒 daily about 10 am–11:30 pm according to Google

Tip: Get the Plaza ticket early and come at sunset.

St Michael’s (Michel) & tower view

The “Michel” is Hamburg’s landmark: a Baroque main church with a tower about 132 metres high and a green copper dome. You can take the lift or stairs to the viewing platform and see the harbour, the Speicherstadt and the Elbe from a bird’s-eye view. Under the church lies a crypt.

Admission and opening times change (check beforehand). Come in the morning when the light is clear.

Tip: Take the lift up and plan the visit on a clear morning.

📍 View in Google Maps

Reeperbahn, St Pauli & Beatles-Platz

The Reeperbahn is Hamburg’s famous entertainment mile: clubs, bars, theatres, arcades and snack bars. The Beatles’ career began here, and you find their traces at the Beatles-Platz and in the side streets (Große Freiheit). By day the district feels quiet, in the evening it fills up.

Keep an eye on bags and phone, avoid lure offers and do not walk alone into dark lanes in the evening. There are many pubs for a beer; many clubs only open late.

Tip: Come in the evening, keep valuables in view and avoid lure offers.

Alster, Jungfernstieg & town hall

The Alster is the reservoir in the middle of the city: around the Binnenalster run promenades, arcades and the Jungfernstieg; the Außenalster is a domain for sailors, rowers and Alster steamers. The town hall on the Rathausmarkt is a neo-Renaissance building with a magnificent interior; tours are possible (check times beforehand).

An Alster cruise takes about an hour. On a sunny day walk around the Außenalster – a good six to seven kilometres.

Tip: Walk around the Außenalster and take a break by the water.

2. Hidden gems

Speicherstadt💎 Hidden gem

The world’s largest historic warehouse district, UNESCO World Heritage since 2015.

The Speicherstadt is a district of red-brick warehouses from the late 19th century, built on oak piles in the Elbe, and has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2015. It was the centre of the trade in coffee, tea, spices and carpets, and carpets and raw materials are still stored in it today. Between the canals there are bridges, gates and towers.

It is most beautiful in the evening when the buildings are lit and reflected in the water. The Miniatur Wunderland and the Hamburg Dungeon are also here. A walk takes about an hour; photographers come at the blue hour.

Tip: Come at the blue hour and pass by the Wasserschloss.

📍 View in Google Maps

Old Elbe Tunnel – on foot under the Elbe💎 Hidden gem

The Old Elbe Tunnel of 1911 is a pedestrian tunnel under the Elbe, about 426 metres long and clad in tiles. You take lifts down and walk through the tunnel to the other side – there waits one of the most beautiful views of the Hamburg skyline from the Steinwerder bank.

The tunnel is free to use on foot (vehicles pay). Plan an hour and go in the evening when the lights come on.

Tip: Walk through the tunnel in the evening and photograph the skyline from the other side.

📍 View in Google Maps

Blankenese – stair quarter & Elbe beach💎 Hidden gem

Blankenese is a former fishing village in the west of Hamburg, on a steep Elbe slope. About 5,000 steps and narrow lanes connect the houses; there are many stair paths, villas and views of the Elbe. On the Elbe beach lies the sand where ships pass close by.

You reach Blankenese by S-Bahn; walk through the stair quarter down to the beach and eat an ice cream by the water. A walk takes two to three hours.

Tip: Take the S-Bahn and walk down the stair quarter to the beach.

📍 View in Google Maps

Planten un Blomen & water-light concerts💎 Hidden gem

Planten un Blomen is a large park in the centre with gardens, ponds, a Japanese garden and roses. In summer water-light concerts take place in the evening: fountains dance to music, brightly lit. Entry to the park is free.

A walk suits a break between museums and harbour. Check the times of the water-light concerts beforehand.

Tip: Come on a summer evening for the water-light concerts.

📍 View in Google Maps

Miniatur Wunderland

The Miniatur Wunderland in the Speicherstadt is the largest model railway in the world: hundreds of trains, airports, cities, landscapes and tiny people over a large area, all with light and time-of-day effects. Even without a passion for railways it is an experience.

You should book tickets online in advance, especially in holidays and at weekends; waiting times can be long. Plan two to three hours.

Tip: Book tickets online and come early in the morning.

📍 View in Google Maps

Hamburg food: fish rolls, labskaus & franzbrötchen

A fish roll with matjes, fried fish or shrimp belongs to Hamburg like the harbour. As a main course labskaus counts: corned beef, beetroot, mashed potato and fried egg – a sailor’s dish. And as pastry the franzbrötchen: a cinnamon-filled roll that Hamburgers love.

Try fish rolls at the Landungsbrücken or in the Portuguese quarter; prices are usually in the single-digit euro range. Ask about seasonal dishes such as Finkenwerder plaice.

Tip: Eat a fish roll at the harbour and a franzbrötchen with coffee.

What makes the north special

A “Moin” works at any time of day. In the Middle Ages the Hanseatic League dominated trade in the North and Baltic Seas. The houses are red brick, the weather changes hourly.

FAQ

When is the fish market?

Very early on Sundays; check times beforehand.

How many days do you need in Hamburg?
Two days for the highlights; with Blankenese and Miniatur Wunderland three.
Is the Hamburg Card worthwhile?
If you travel a lot by train and plan several admissions, often yes; calculate beforehand.
