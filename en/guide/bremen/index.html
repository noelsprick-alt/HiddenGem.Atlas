Getting there and around

You reach Bremen by train from Hamburg in about an hour; old town on foot.

Bremen is well connected: the main station is central, from there you walk to the market square in a few minutes. The old town is compact and easy to explore on foot; for the Viertel and the Bürgerpark you take tram or bus. Check timetables beforehand.

Bremen airport is close to the city.

1. Must-sees

Schnoor quarter

Narrow lanes, small shops, cafés. 4.7 stars on Google from over 20,000 reviews. Visitors advise a Saturday since much is closed on Sundays.

The Schnoor quarter is Bremen’s oldest surviving residential district: small, narrow houses from the 15th and 16th centuries stand close together in narrow lanes. Today you find cafés, crafts, goldsmiths and small restaurants here. The name probably comes from the “Schnüre”, the ropes of the former rope-makers.

A walk in the morning is most beautiful when the lanes are still empty. Many shops are closed on Sundays (check beforehand).

Tip: Come in the morning or on a Saturday.

Roland on the market square

Since 1404 the stone Roland has watched over the city’s freedom.

The Roland on the market square is a stone figure about ten metres high from 1404 and is regarded as a symbol of Bremen’s freedom and market rights. Together with the town hall it is a UNESCO World Heritage site. Opposite stands St Peter’s Cathedral with its towers.

The market square is the heart of the city; around it are cafés and the Bremen Town Musicians.

Tip: See the town hall and Roland together.

Bremen Town Musicians

The bronze figure of the Town Musicians next to the town hall shows donkey, dog, cat and rooster on top of each other. The custom: hold the donkey’s front hooves with both hands and make a wish – hence its shiny hooves. The fairy tale by the Brothers Grimm is world famous.

Come early or late when fewer people are there.

Tip: Hold the hooves and make a wish.

Town hall & cathedral

Bremen’s town hall is a Renaissance building with a richly decorated façade and, with the Roland, a UNESCO World Heritage site. Inside you can join a tour and see magnificent halls (check times beforehand). St Peter’s Cathedral invites you to climb the tower.

Plan an hour for town hall and cathedral.

Tip: Book a town hall tour.

📍 View in Google Maps

Schlachte & Weser promenade

The Schlachte is the promenade on the Weser with beer gardens, restaurants and ships. In summer people sit by the water, in winter there is a Christmas market and “Schlachte-Zauber” (check dates beforehand). From the water you see old warehouses and ships.

Come in the late afternoon and stay until sunset.

Tip: Come in the late afternoon.

Das Viertel – pubs & culture💎 Hidden gem

Das Viertel, also called “Ostertor/Steintor”, is Bremen’s lively scene district with cafés, pubs, arthouse cinemas and shops. At weekends the streets are full in the evening.

For an evening with beer and music it is the best place.

Tip: Come on weekend evenings.

📍 View in Google Maps

Wallanlagen & mill on the ramparts💎 Hidden gem

The Wallanlagen are a green park on the former city fortifications with ponds, paths and a windmill. They suit a quiet walk in the middle of the city.

Combine the walk with a coffee by the water.

Tip: Combine the walk with a coffee.

📍 View in Google Maps

Bremen specialities: coffee, kale & pinkel

Bremen is a coffee city (roasting tradition). As a dish there is kale with pinkel (smoked groats sausage) in winter, knipp and Bremer Klaben (raisin bread). Try a beer from Beck’s or a regional craft beer with it.

Prices change; ask beforehand.

Tip: Try kale with pinkel in winter.
