Getting there and around

Gran Canaria has a large airport in the east; buses link the towns. For mountains and harbour villages a rental car or a tour is practical; roads are winding.

Gran Canaria Airport lies on the east coast; from there you reach the south in about 30 to 40 minutes and Las Palmas in about 20 minutes (check prices and timetables beforehand). Global buses connect the main places, but for the mountain villages a rental car is worthwhile.

The roads in the island’s interior are winding; plan more time than the map shows.

1. Must-sees

Dunes of Maspalomas🆓 Free to experience

Sunrise in the dunes. Bring water and sunscreen and avoid the midday heat.

The dunes of Maspalomas are a nature reserve at the southern tip of the island: golden sand in waves, the sea behind and a small palm grove with a lagoon. A walk at sunrise is most beautiful, when the wind has smoothed the surface and the shadows are long. At midday it becomes hot and shadeless.

Stick to the marked paths, take plenty of water and wear sun protection. At the end of the dunes lies Playa del Inglés beach; the Maspalomas lighthouse is a good meeting point.

Tip: Come before sunrise and bring water.

Roque Nublo🆓 Free to experience

The rock finger at about 1,800 metres in the centre. The car park is small – bus plus hike is a good alternative.

The Roque Nublo is a basalt rock about 80 metres high at roughly 1,800 metres and the landmark of the island. The path from the La Goleta car park takes about two to three hours there and back and is mostly easy to walk, but stony in places. From the top you look over a sea of clouds, mountains and, in clear weather, as far as the Teide on Tenerife.

The weather changes quickly up there: take a jacket even if the sun shines below. Plan the climb in the morning, when the clouds often still lie below you. Parking is limited (check access and rules beforehand).

Tip: Start in the morning and take a jacket.

Puerto de Mogán🆓 Free to experience

Fish in the harbour at night.

Puerto de Mogán in the south-west is called “Little Venice”: white-washed houses with bougainvillea, small canals and bridges, a marina and a beach promenade. The place is calmer than the tourist centres in the south. On Friday there is a market selling crafts, textiles and souvenirs (check the day beforehand).

In the evening you can eat fresh fish in the harbour; prices are usually touristy but the atmosphere is lovely. A boat also runs here from Puerto Rico (check timetable beforehand).

Tip: Come in the evening for fish and stroll through the canals.

Las Palmas: Vegueta & Triana

The capital Las Palmas has a colonial old town, Vegueta: cobblestones, squares, the Santa Ana Cathedral and the Columbus House (Casa de Colón). In the neighbouring district of Triana there are pedestrian zones, shops and cafés. Further north lies Las Canteras beach with a long promenade.

Plan half a day to a whole day. The lanes of Vegueta are quiet in the morning. Check opening times and admission for the museums beforehand.

Tip: Start in the morning in Vegueta and end at Las Canteras beach.

Teror & Gran Canaria’s mountain villages🆓 Free to experience

Teror is a mountain village with wooden balconies, cobblestones and a basilica where the island’s patron saint is venerated. On Sundays a market takes place selling sausages, cheese and pastries (check the day beforehand). The drive from Las Palmas takes about 30 to 40 minutes.

Try the local chorizo de Teror and fresh bread. Combine the visit with a drive into the green mountains in the north.

Tip: Come on Sunday for the market.

2. Hidden gem

Cueva Pintada in Gáldar💎 Hidden gem

A place most beach holidaymakers never see: the painted cave of the ancient Canarians. My tip: the guided tour (about €6).

The Cueva Pintada in Gáldar is an archaeological site of the ancient Canarians: a painted cave with geometric patterns in red, black and white, regarded as an early culture of the island. A museum and an archaeological park explain daily life and culture of the indigenous people.

Find out beforehand about guided tours, languages and access. Plan one to two hours.

🕒 Tue–Sun about 10 am–6 pm, closed Mondays

Tip: Book the guided tour and check languages.

Agaete, Dedo de Dios & Puerto de las Nieves💎 Hidden gem

In the north-west lies Agaete, a village with white houses, cobblestones and a harbour, Puerto de las Nieves. The “Dedo de Dios”, the “Finger of God”, was a rock in the sea that broke off in a storm in 2005; the remains and the monument can still be seen. At the harbour there are fish restaurants with fresh produce, and natural rock pools invite you to swim.

The coastal road there is winding but beautiful. Come at midday to eat fish.

Tip: Come for lunch and swim in the rock pools.

Tejeda & Artenara – cave houses in the mountains💎 Hidden gem

The mountain village of Tejeda is among the most beautiful villages in Spain and lies in the centre of the island. Here you try almond pastries and almond cake. Further north lies Artenara, the highest village on the island, where many people live in cave houses; a cave chapel and viewpoints are worth a stop.

The roads are winding; allow enough time and drive carefully. Take a jacket, as it is noticeably cooler than on the coast.

Tip: Try almond pastries and take a jacket.

Fataga valley & Arteara💎 Hidden gem

The Fataga valley is a green valley with palms, white villages and rock formations. Near Arteara lies an important necropolis of the indigenous people with burial mounds. The area is quiet and offers short hikes and views.

Plan the drive as part of a loop from Maspalomas into the island’s interior. Check opening times and access to the archaeological site beforehand.

Tip: Combine the valley with the Roque Nublo into a day trip.

Playa de Amadores & southern beaches🆓 Free to experience

Playa de Amadores is an artificially created beach with light sand and shallow, calm water in a sheltered bay – good for families. Other beaches in the south are Playa del Inglés, Maspalomas and Puerto Rico. The south has the most reliable sun on the island.

Loungers and parasols cost money; check prices beforehand. Watch the flags and currents.

Tip: Come in the morning, when it is quieter.

Papas arrugadas & mojo – Canarian cuisine

Canarian cuisine is simple and tasty: papas arrugadas are small potatoes boiled in salt water with a wrinkled skin, served with red or green mojo sauce. Also try gofio (roasted flour), goat cheese, fish and bienmesabe (almond cream).

Small places in mountain villages and harbour towns usually serve the classics cheaper than beach restaurants. Prices change; ask beforehand.

Tip: Eat in small places in the mountain villages.

What makes Gran Canaria special

Long before the Spaniards the ancient Canarians lived here. At the end of the 15th century Castile conquered the island. In 1492 Columbus landed in Las Palmas on his first voyage to America.

FAQ

Do I need a rental car?

Practical for the mountains; buses serve many places.

How many days do you need on Gran Canaria?
For beach and mountains five to seven days are good; for the highlights three suffice.
When is the best time to go?
The island is mild all year; the south is usually sunnier, the north greener and cloudier.
