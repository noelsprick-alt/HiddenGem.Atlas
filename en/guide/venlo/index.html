Getting there and around

You reach Venlo from NRW by regional train or car in a short time; you walk the old town.

From Düsseldorf, Duisburg or Mönchengladbach you reach Venlo by regional train in about an hour; the station is roughly 10 minutes’ walk from the old town (check timetables beforehand). By car you come via the A61 and park in the car parks on the edge.

A bike makes the surroundings easier: rental bikes are available at the station. The old town is small and easy to explore on foot.

1. Must-sees

Market and Stadhuis

The market square with the town hall is the heart of the city.

The Stadhuis on the market square dates from 1597 and is a Renaissance building with stepped gable, columned portal and a carillon that sounds at certain times. It is one of the most beautiful buildings in the city and a popular photo motif.

Around the market lie cafés with terraces, small shops and the lanes of the old town. Sit down, order a coffee and watch the bustle – that is the core of the Venlo day. In summer it is especially lively here.

Coffee on the Meuse

A coffee on the Meuse.

On the Maasboulevard you can stroll along the river and stop at cafés and restaurants with a view of the water. From here you see the bridges over the Maas, barges and walkers. If you have time, walk a bit along the bank into quieter areas.

The Maas is broad and calm here – perfect for a break. A piece of vlaai (Limburg fruit pie) with it rounds off the coffee.

Fries with frietsaus

Afterwards a cone of fries with frietsaus.

Chips are part of everyday culture in the Netherlands: you get them in a paper cone with mayo (fritessaus) or other sauces. The “patatje oorlog” comes with mayo, peanut sauce and onions, the “patatje speciaal” with mayo, curry ketchup and onions.

Ask about size if you do not want to eat alone – portions are often large. Sit on a bench at the market or by the Maas and eat them hot. Prices change, expect a few euros per portion.

Sint-Martinuskerk

The Gothic Sint-Martinuskerk from the 15th century is the landmark of Venlo. Its tower is visible from far away, and inside you find a mixture of Gothic architecture and Baroque as well as works of art and tombs. The church and tower are not always open – check times beforehand.

On the square in front there are small cafés where you can take a break. The walk from the market to the church takes only a few minutes.

Tip: Also look at the side streets around the church – it is quieter here than at the market.

Limburg Museum

The Limburgs Museum in the Keulsepoort tells the history of the province of Limburg from prehistory to today. You see finds from Roman times, medieval objects and everyday items. A good choice in the rain.

Plan about one to two hours. Admission and opening times change, please check beforehand.

Tip: Perfect for a rain shower in the old town.

Museum van Bommel van Dam

The museum shows modern and contemporary art in a bright building by the city park. The collection includes works by artists of the region and international names, plus temporary exhibitions. It is quiet and good for a break from the old town.

Check opening times and admission beforehand; the museum is often closed on Mondays.

Tip: Combine it with a walk through the city park.

Shopping & lanes: Vleesstraat and Lomstraat

The Vleesstraat and Lomstraat are the shopping streets of the old town: chain stores, small boutiques, cafés and bakeries. Many guests from Germany come exactly for this. Allow time for a stroll and let the lanes carry you.

On Sundays many shops are closed or open later; check this beforehand if you want to shop.

Tip: Plan a stroll in the morning and the break at the market at noon.

FAQ

Is Venlo worth a day trip?

Yes, a relaxed trip just across the border.

Is one day enough in Venlo?
Yes – one day is enough for the old town; with the Maasduinen or Arcen plan two.
Do I need euros in Venlo?
Yes, the Netherlands uses the euro; cards are accepted almost everywhere, cash is rarely needed.
