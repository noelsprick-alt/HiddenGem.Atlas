Getting there and around

Vilnius has its own airport; coaches link the city to Riga and Warsaw. Check current connections beforehand.

Vilnius airport lies close to the centre; bus, train and taxi bring you into the city in a few minutes (check prices beforehand). The old town is compact and easy to explore on foot. Lithuania uses the euro.

For Trakai and the Hill of Crosses you need train/bus or a tour or car.

1. Must-sees

Gediminas Tower

Up the tower at sunset. Admission is about €8, the funicular about €2, and a 360° view at the top.

The Gediminas Tower is the remnant of the Upper Castle on the castle hill and the landmark of Vilnius. From the platform at the top you see the old town with its red roofs, the Neris and modern skyscrapers. You ascend on foot by a path or by funicular; inside the tower there is a small exhibition.

At sunset the view is most beautiful. Opening times and prices change, please check beforehand.

🕒 daily about 10 am–8 pm

Tip: Come at sunset and take the footpath up.

Užupis🆓 Free to experience

A walk through the self-proclaimed artists’ republic – with its own constitution on a wall.

Užupis is an artists’ district across the river Vilnia that declared itself a republic in 1997 – with president, flag and a constitution in many languages on wall plaques. The articles are humorous and philosophical, for example about cats, dogs and the right to happiness. On 1 April Užupis celebrates its independence day.

You find galleries, cafés and the “Angel of Užupis” on the main square. Plan one to two hours.

Tip: Read the constitution on the wall and have a coffee in the district.

Vilnius Old Town🆓 Free to experience

Vilnius Old Town is a UNESCO World Heritage site and one of the largest old towns in Eastern Europe. Baroque churches, narrow lanes, courtyards and squares alternate. The cathedral on Cathedral Square with its bell tower is a meeting point.

The old town is easy to explore on foot. Plan half a day to a whole day and drift into courtyards and side streets.

Tip: Drift into courtyards and side streets.

Cathedral & Cathedral Square🆓 Free to experience

The neoclassical cathedral with its columned façade stands on Cathedral Square, the heart of the city. Beside it rises the bell tower. On the square lies a stone with the word “Stebuklas” (“miracle”); custom says to turn around three times on the stone and make a wish.

The interior of the cathedral is freely accessible; check times beforehand.

Tip: Turn around three times on the Stebuklas stone and make a wish.

Gate of Dawn (Aušros Vartai)

The Gate of Dawn is the only surviving gate of the city’s fortifications. In a chapel above the gate hangs a revered image of Mary that draws pilgrims from many countries. The passage is busy, and believers often stop and pray.

Be respectful and dress modestly. Check times beforehand.

Tip: Be respectful and dress modestly.

Vilnius University & courtyards

Vilnius University was founded in 1579 and is among the oldest in Eastern Europe. A complex of courtyards, arcades and St John’s Church with its bell tower. You can visit the courtyards, where frescoes and historic rooms are located; a ticket may be needed for the church and tower (check beforehand).

A walk through the courtyards is a quiet break.

Tip: Walk through the courtyards and climb the bell tower.

Trakai – island water castle💎 Hidden gem

About 30 kilometres west of Vilnius lies Trakai, a red-brick castle on an island in Lake Galvė. You can enter via a wooden bridge, discover history in the museum and try kibinai in the town – filled pastries of the Karaites.

Train or bus take about 30 to 45 minutes (check timetables beforehand). In summer you can rent boats.

Tip: Take the train and try kibinai.

Three Crosses Hill & viewpoint💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

On a hill east of the old town stand three white concrete crosses, a memorial with history. From there you have a beautiful view over the old town, especially at sunset. The climb takes about 10 to 15 minutes.

A cheap, quiet viewpoint with no admission.

Tip: Go up at sunset.

Lithuanian cuisine: cepelinai, šaltibarščiai & kibinai

Lithuanian cuisine is hearty: cepelinai are large potato dumplings with meat or cheese filling and bacon-cream sauce; šaltibarščiai is a cold pink beetroot soup with kefir that refreshes in summer; with it dark bread, kibinai and beer. For something sweet try šakotis, a tree cake.

Traditional places often serve the dishes at moderate prices; ask about lunch menus. Prices change.

Tip: Ask about lunch menus and try cepelinai.

What makes Lithuania special

Lithuania is the country where basketball is almost a religion and where you eat kepta duona (fried garlic bread) with beer. According to legend Grand Duke Gediminas dreamed of an iron wolf and then founded the city. In 1989 two million people formed a human chain from Vilnius to Tallinn, and in 1990 Lithuania declared independence as the first Soviet republic.

FAQ

How do I get to the Hill of Crosses?

By rental car or tour; it lies near Šiauliai.

How many days do you need in Vilnius?
Two days for the old town, Užupis and museums; with Trakai three.
Do I pay in euros?
Yes, Lithuania uses the euro.
