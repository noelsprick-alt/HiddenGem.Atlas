My story in New York

I was there – and the best part: many of the city’s most impressive experiences cost nothing at all. My tip: walk the Brooklyn Bridge towards Manhattan early in the morning, then along the High Line and lunch at Chelsea Market. In the evening choose Williamsburg over Times Square. And for the Statue of Liberty: the free Staten Island Ferry instead of an expensive boat tour.

Brooklyn Bridge in the morning🆓 Free to experience

Walk across the bridge towards Manhattan early in the morning.

The High Line🆓 Free to experience

The former elevated railway turned park, about 2.5 km between the Meatpacking District and Hudson Yards, free.

Chelsea Market

Central Park🆓 Free to experience

Central Park is Manhattan’s green lung: lawns, lakes, bridges, sculptures and paths. Bethesda Terrace, Bow Bridge and Strawberry Fields are well-known points. You can rent bikes or walk; a walk takes two to three hours.

Start in the morning and take water. In autumn the foliage is especially beautiful.

Tip: Start in the morning at Bethesda Terrace.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Views: Top of the Rock, Empire State & Summit

New York has several viewpoints: Top of the Rock at Rockefeller Center (with a view of the Empire State Building), the Empire State Building itself and Summit One Vanderbilt. Tickets are expensive; compare prices and book online. The view is most beautiful at sunset.

If you want to save money choose just one platform.

Tip: Choose one platform and book for sunset.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Times Square & Broadway🆓 Free to experience

Times Square is a sea of neon signs, loud and crowded – see it once, best in the evening. Around the square lie the Broadway theatres. Tickets for musicals and plays are available online or, sometimes discounted, at box offices (check prices beforehand).

Watch for pickpockets and pushy offers.

Tip: Come in the evening and see a Broadway show.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Metropolitan Museum of Art

The Met is one of the largest art museums in the world with Egyptian temples, European paintings, armour and modern art. It is huge; choose two to three areas. On the roof there is a garden with a view of Central Park in summer (check times and prices beforehand).

Plan three to four hours.

Tip: Choose two to three areas.

Phone-booth speakeasy💎 Hidden gem

You reach a speakeasy through a phone booth in a hot dog shop – on my map; check reservations and current status beforehand.

Roosevelt Island Tramway💎 Hidden gem

The cable car to Roosevelt Island crosses the East River and offers views of Manhattan for the price of a normal subway ticket. On the island there are parks and calm promenades. It is a cheap alternative to expensive observation decks.

Check fare and operating times beforehand.

Tip: Ride there at sunset.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

DUMBO & Brooklyn Bridge Park💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

In DUMBO under the Manhattan Bridge there is the famous photo spot on Washington Street with a bridge view. Brooklyn Bridge Park offers promenades, piers and a view of the skyline. Cafés and shops make the district pleasant.

Come early in the morning when fewer people are there.

Tip: Come early in the morning for the photo.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Greenwich Village & Washington Square💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

Greenwich Village is a quiet district with brick houses, small cafés, jazz clubs and Washington Square Park with its triumphal arch. Street musicians play here, and students from the nearby university sit in the park.

A good place for a relaxed afternoon.

Tip: Spend the afternoon in the park and cafés.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

New York classics: bagel, pizza slice & deli

Try a bagel with cream cheese, a pizza slice for a few dollars, a pastrami sandwich in a deli and cheesecake. Many classics are cheap and fast. Prices change; note tipping rules (18–20 percent is usual in restaurants).

Ask locals for the best pizza corner.

Tip: Budget for tipping in restaurants.

What makes New York special

Before it was New York it was called New Amsterdam. In 1664 the English took over. Through Ellis Island about twelve million immigrants came to the USA between 1892 and 1954.
