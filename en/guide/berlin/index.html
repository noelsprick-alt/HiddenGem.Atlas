My story in Berlin

I was in Berlin, and exactly this mix makes the city. My tip: Mauerpark on a Sunday – flea market and karaoke in the amphitheatre are Berlin at its purest. Add street food at Markthalle Neun and Friedrichshain around the RAW site at night. And once cycle across the runway of Tempelhofer Feld.

Getting there and around

You reach Berlin by ICE, flight (BER) or coach. In the city: S-Bahn, U-Bahn, tram and bicycle.

BER airport is connected to the centre by airport express and S-Bahn; the main station lies in the middle of the government district. In the city you use S-Bahn, U-Bahn, tram and bus (note ticket zones A/B/C). Check timetables and fares beforehand.

Berlin is big; plan distances realistically.

1. Must-sees

Mauerpark on a Sunday

Brandenburg Gate & Reichstag

The Brandenburg Gate is Berlin’s landmark and a symbol of German division and unity. Very close stands the Reichstag building with its glass dome, which you can visit after registration (online registration needed beforehand). The view from above over the government district is worthwhile.

Plan the Reichstag visit in advance. Check times and rules beforehand.

Tip: Register beforehand for the dome.

📍 View in Google Maps

East Side Gallery

The East Side Gallery is a roughly 1.3-kilometre remnant of the Berlin Wall painted by artists from around the world. The famous brotherly-kiss painting can be seen here. The wall is freely accessible. Come early to avoid groups.

Respect the artworks and do not write on them.

Tip: Come early in the morning.

📍 View in Google Maps

Berlin Wall Memorial

On Bernauer Straße lies the memorial with a preserved wall strip, viewing tower and documentation centre. Here you learn how the division separated families and what escape attempts there were. The visit is free.

Plan one to two hours.

Tip: Plan one to two hours.

📍 View in Google Maps

Museum Island

Museum Island is a UNESCO World Heritage site with several museums, including the Pergamon Museum (partly closed for renovation – check the current status beforehand), the Neues Museum with the bust of Nefertiti and the Alte Nationalgalerie. Around the island lie the cathedral and the Spree.

Choose one or two museums. Book tickets online.

Tip: Choose one or two museums and book online.

📍 View in Google Maps

Currywurst, döner & Berlin cuisine

Berlin has its own classics: currywurst with fries, döner (according to tradition invented in Berlin), Berliner pancakes and meatballs. At markets like the Markthalle Neun there is street food from all over the world. Prices change; ask beforehand.

Try both and ask locals for recommendations.

Tip: Compare currywurst and döner.

2. Crazy places

Teufelsberg💎 Hidden gem

A mountain of war rubble with an abandoned US listening station on top. 4.3 stars on Google. Visitors mention about €12 admission and a forest path from Grunewald station.

📍 View in Google Maps

Spreepark & pool in the Spree💎 Hidden gem

Spreepark, the GDR amusement park whose operator fled to Peru – and a pool floating in the Spree as an old ship hull. Both on my map.

📍 View in Google Maps

Kreuzberg & Neukölln – exploring the Kieze💎 Hidden gem

Kreuzberg and Neukölln are multicultural districts with cafés, bars, markets and street art. The Turkish Market on Maybachufer (Tue and Fri) is a highlight (check times beforehand). The area is lively, full in the evening and sometimes loud.

Set off in the afternoon and drift.

Tip: Come to the Maybachufer market.

📍 View in Google Maps

Berlin club scene💎 Hidden gem

Berlin is famous for its nightlife; many clubs have strict door policies and photo rules. If you want to party, find out beforehand about the club’s style and rules. There are also bars and live music without a strict door.

Be respectful, bring cash and plan flexibly.

Tip: Find out the rules beforehand and be respectful.

📍 View in Google Maps

Tiergarten & Victory Column💎 Hidden gem

The Tiergarten is the large city park in the centre. In the middle of the park stands the Victory Column, which you can climb. Paths lead through meadows and woods; a walk is a good break.

Combine the visit with the Brandenburg Gate and Reichstag.

Tip: Climb the Victory Column.

📍 View in Google Maps

What makes Berlin special

In 1948/49 the Allies supplied West Berlin via the Airlift. In 1961 the Wall was built, about 155 kilometres long; on 9 November 1989 it fell. The currywurst is also a Berlin child: Herta Heuwer sold it for the first time in 1949.
