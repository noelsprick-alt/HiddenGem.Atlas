My story in Düsseldorf

I am there often – and these are five things that keep surprising me. My favourite day to unwind: Flingern. The Saturday market at Hermannplatz, cake at Café Hüftgold, browsing LANGBRETT in the old petrol station and The SKAND Klub at night. No sightseeing, just a district that feels like home. In May 2025 I was in the middle of Japan Day – Japan on my doorstep. I was in Japan in 2022, and on Japan Day I had exactly that feeling again for a few hours: that mix of order, playfulness and friendliness.

Getting there and around

Düsseldorf has an airport and a large central station; within the city run subway, tram and bus. You walk the old town.

Düsseldorf lies centrally in the Rhineland: the main station is a hub for ICE and regional trains, the airport lies north of the city. You reach the old town on foot or by tram and U-Bahn. Check timetables beforehand.

Bike and walking are often easiest in the city centre.

1. Must-sees & five surprises

The wheel-turners

The city symbol is children turning cartwheels – according to legend because of the victory in the Battle of Worringen in 1288. You find small figures on many corners.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Rhine Tower – a clock

The light points on the shaft show the time (the world’s largest decimal clock).

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Little Tokyo & Japan Day

Around Immermannstraße lives one of the largest Japanese communities in Europe; in May there is Japan Day with fireworks. Plan: drift along the Rhine bank by day, lunch on Immermannstraße, secure a spot at the water in good time in the evening.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Altbier and the Köbes

In the old town’s brewery pubs the Köbes serves you – a waiter with a blue apron and a loose tongue. He puts down a new alt unasked; a beer mat on the glass ends the game.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Old Town & “longest bar in the world”🆓 Free to experience

Düsseldorf’s old town is known as the “longest bar in the world”: hundreds of pubs and brewhouses in a small space. Here you drink Altbier, a dark top-fermented beer, in traditional houses like Uerige or Schumacher. The lanes are full in the evening.

Drink slowly and enjoy the atmosphere.

Tip: Try Altbier in a traditional brewhouse.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Rhine promenade & MedienHafen🆓 Free to experience

The Rhine promenade is the strolling mile on the river with a view of bridges and ships. The MedienHafen offers modern architecture, including the curved Gehry buildings, restaurants and bars. A walk leads there in about 30 minutes.

Come in the late afternoon and stay until evening.

Tip: Come in the late afternoon.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Königsallee (“Kö”)

The Königsallee is the well-known shopping street with luxury shops and a city moat with bridges. Even without shopping a walk is worthwhile. At the end lies the Hofgarten.

Stroll from the Hofgarten to the old town.

Tip: Stroll to the Hofgarten.

2. Hidden gems

Flingern – my favourite district💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

On Saturday across the market at Hermannplatz, cake at Café Hüftgold, browsing LANGBRETT in the old petrol station and The SKAND Klub at night.

EKŌ-Haus in Niederkassel💎 Hidden gem

A Japanese Buddhist temple with garden and tea house. Reserve for the wooden house; cash only.

🕒 Tue–Sat about 1–5 pm

Carlsplatz & market💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

Carlsplatz is a market square in the old town with stalls for fruit, vegetables, delicacies and flowers. Cafés and snack bars make it a good place for a break (check market days beforehand).

Come in the morning.

Tip: Come in the morning.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Hofgarten & Benrath Palace💎 Hidden gem

The Hofgarten is Germany’s oldest public park in the centre. Benrath Palace in the south is a Rococo palace with park and pond, reachable by train or tram (check opening times beforehand).

Combine park and palace into half a day.

Tip: Plan half a day for the palace.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Japanese cuisine: ramen, sushi & bakeries

Around Immermannstraße you find ramen shops, sushi restaurants, bakeries and supermarkets. Try ramen, onigiri and Japanese pastries. Prices change; ask beforehand.

Come at lunch for lunch menus.

Tip: Come at lunch for lunch menus.

Why Düsseldorf is so Japanese

From the 1950s Japanese companies opened their European headquarters on the Rhine. With the companies came families, schools and restaurants. Japan Day has existed since 2002.
