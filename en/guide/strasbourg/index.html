Getting there and around

From Freiburg you are in Alsace in a few minutes across the Rhine; you reach Strasbourg by train.

From Germany you reach Strasbourg by train (for example via Karlsruhe or Offenburg) in about 1.5 to 3 hours, depending on your starting point (check timetables beforehand). The old town is an island in the Ill, compact and explorable on foot; trams help over longer distances.

By car it is best to park at a park-and-ride on the edge and take the tram. Strasbourg is also a good starting point for the Alsace wine route.

1. Must-sees

La Petite France

Through the half-timbered quarter in the morning.

La Petite France is the former tanners’ and fishermen’s district on an island in the Ill: half-timbered houses with flower boxes, narrow lanes and water canals shape the picture. The name probably comes from a former hospital for syphilis patients (“French disease”), not from France as a whole – a detail guides like to tell.

The district is most beautiful in the early morning, before the tour groups arrive; then the houses are reflected in the water and you hear almost only birds. Also walk to Rue du Bain-aux-Plantes and Place Benjamin Zix, where you find the classic photo motifs.

Tip: Come before 9 a.m. – then you have lanes and canals almost to yourself.

Barrage Vauban🆓 Free to experience

The roof terrace with a view of Petite France and the cathedral – free. Open until about 7 pm.

The Barrage Vauban is a covered bridge from the late 17th century, built as part of the city fortifications. On the roof lies a terrace that you can enter free of charge. From there you have a view of the Ill, La Petite France, the Covered Bridges and the cathedral.

Inside you see sculptures from the city’s museum. The terrace is open until about early evening (check times beforehand); sunset is especially beautiful.

Tip: Go up shortly before sunset – then the light lies warm on the cathedral.

Strasbourg Cathedral (Notre-Dame)🆓 Free to experience

The cathedral of red Vosges sandstone is a masterpiece of Gothic. With its tower about 142 metres high it was the tallest building in the world until 1874. The filigree façade, the rose window and the colourful windows inside are impressive. You can climb to a viewing platform (about 330 steps, admission; check times beforehand).

From above you survey the old town, the Rhine and the Vosges. The façade is most beautiful in the late afternoon when the light makes the stone glow. Plan an hour for the interior and one for the tower.

Tip: Combine the tower with the astronomical clock.

Place Kléber, Place Gutenberg & Maison Kammerzell🆓 Free to experience

Place Kléber is the great central square of the city where trams, cafés and the Christmas tree come together. A few steps away lies Place Gutenberg with the statue of the printing pioneer who worked in Strasbourg. On the cathedral square stands the Maison Kammerzell, an elaborate half-timbered house with a richly carved façade, which houses a restaurant.

A walk between these squares is easy, flat and worthwhile at any time of day. Sit in a café and watch the bustle.

Tip: Go in the evening when the squares are lit.

Palais Rohan & museums

The Palais Rohan next to the cathedral is a Baroque palace from the 18th century that today houses three museums: archaeology, decorative arts and fine arts. The rooms are splendidly furnished; paintings, furniture and porcelain tell of the cardinal’s life.

Plan one to two hours; a visit is worthwhile in the rain or if you want to learn more about the history of the region. Admission and opening times change, please check.

Tip: A perfect rainy-day tip right next to the cathedral.

Boat tour on the Ill

A boat tour on the Ill lasts about an hour and leads through locks, past La Petite France, the Covered Bridges, the European Quarter and the Palais de l’Europe. You see the city from a perspective you cannot get on foot.

There are audio guides in several languages. Check prices and departure times beforehand; in summer the boats are often full, so book early.

Tip: Book in the morning, then the light is lovely and the boats less full.

2. Hidden gems

Astronomical clock in the cathedral💎 Hidden gem

Every day at 12:30 the apostles pass by and a rooster crows – on my map.

The astronomical clock in Strasbourg Cathedral was built in the 16th century and renewed in the 19th century. It shows time, moon phases, constellations and many astronomical data. Every day at 12:30 a figure play starts: the apostles pass before Christ, and a rooster crows.

Because many visitors come, you should be there early and secure a spot. The clock is in the cathedral; a small admission is charged for the figure play (check prices and times beforehand).

Tip: Be there 20–30 minutes before 12:30, otherwise you stand in the second row.

Neustadt – the German quarter💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

After 1871 the German Empire built a new district east of the old town, the Neustadt, with broad streets, villas and prestigious buildings such as the Imperial Palace (Palais du Rhin) and the university. The architecture looks German and contrasts with the old town; since 2017 the Neustadt is part of the UNESCO World Heritage.

A walk through the Neustadt is quieter than in the old town and good for architecture fans. Plan one to two hours and eat in a café afterwards.

Tip: Walk from Place de la République towards the university.

Jardin des Deux Rives – bridge to Germany💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

A border garden on the Rhine: a curved pedestrian bridge leads over the river to Kehl in Germany. The park is a lovely place for walking, jogging and picnics. From the bridge you see cargo ships and the water.

You get there by tram or bike. It is a good place to find some quiet if the old town is too crowded. Plan an hour.

Tip: Take the tram to Port du Rhin and walk across the bridge.

Winstub food: flammkuchen, choucroute & baeckeoffe💎 Hidden gem

Alsatian cuisine is hearty: flammkuchen (tarte flambée) with bacon and onions, choucroute garnie (sauerkraut with meat), baeckeoffe (stew with potatoes and meat in a clay pot) and Munster cheese. You eat them in a winstub – traditional places with wood panelling and lots of character.

Alsatian white wine goes well with the food, for example Riesling or Gewürztraminer. Reserve at weekends; ask about the daily menu, which is often cheaper.

Tip: Reserve at weekends and try the daily-menu suggestion.

Christmas market – Capitale de Noël💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

Strasbourg calls itself the “Capital of Christmas”; the Christkindelsmärik has existed since the 16th century and is among the oldest Christmas markets in Europe. Around the cathedral and on several squares stand stalls with mulled wine, bredele (biscuits), handicrafts and decoration.

In December the city is very crowded – especially at weekends. Come on a weekday or early in the day, book accommodation in good time and plan time for the lights in the evening.

Tip: Come on a weekday and early in the day; stay in the evening for the lights.

What makes Strasbourg special

Between 1870 and 1945 Strasbourg changed nationality four times – today the European Parliament meets here.

FAQ

Is Strasbourg worth a day trip?

Yes, especially from Freiburg.

How many days do you need in Strasbourg?
One long day is enough for the old town, two days with Neustadt, museums and boat.
Do I pay in euros?
Yes, France uses the euro; cards are accepted almost everywhere.
