Getting to Brussels

From NRW you take the train (Thalys/ICE network) or coach to Brussels; check current connections beforehand. In the centre you walk most sights.

Brussels is a hub for Thalys/Eurostar, ICE and regional trains: the Brussels-Midi and Brussels-Central stations are central. Zaventem airport is about 20 minutes from the centre by train (check timetables beforehand). The city centre is easy to explore on foot; metro, tram and bus complement.

On languages: French and Dutch are official languages; in the tourist area you get by well with English.

1. Must-sees

Grand-Place🆓 Free to experience

Ornate guildhalls and the Town Hall. Particularly lovely when lit up in the evening.

The Grand-Place is one of Europe’s most beautiful market squares and a UNESCO World Heritage site. Around the square stand magnificent guild houses with golden decoration, plus the Gothic town hall with its tall tower. Every two years in August the square is covered with a flower carpet (check the date beforehand).

In the evening the square is lit and especially atmospheric. Come early in the morning when fewer people are there.

Tip: Come early in the morning or in the evening for the lighting.

Atomium

Built for the 1958 World Expo. The view from the top is worth it, but expect waits at the lift.

🕒 daily about 10 am–6 pm

Manneken Pis, Jeanneke Pis & Zinneke Pis🆓 Free to experience

The Manneken Pis is a small bronze figure of a peeing boy and the city’s landmark. He has a large wardrobe and is dressed in costumes on certain days. Less well known are the Jeanneke Pis (a girl) and the Zinneke Pis (a dog) at other places.

The figure is smaller than many expect – take it with humour. Check the costume dates beforehand.

Tip: Take it with humour – the figure is small.

Waffles, fries & chocolate

Brussels is known for waffles (the Brussels one is rectangular and airy, the Liège waffle denser and caramelised), fries with mayonnaise and pralines. Many shops lie around the Galeries Royales Saint-Hubert and the Grand-Place.

Prices in tourist areas are high; try several shops and ask about flavours. Prices change.

Tip: Compare Brussels and Liège waffles.

Belgian beer

Belgium has a great beer tradition: Trappist beers, lambic and gueuze (spontaneously fermented), dubbel, tripel and many more. In Brussels pubs you find menus with hundreds of kinds. Mind the alcohol content – many beers are strong.

A brewery tour or a visit to a lambic brewery shows how the beers are made (check times and prices beforehand).

Tip: Ask for a recommendation and mind the strength.

Comic museum & comic murals

Brussels is the capital of comics: Tintin, the Smurfs and Lucky Luke come from Belgium. The Comic Museum (Belgian Comic Strip Center) is housed in an Art Nouveau building. In the city a comic mural trail runs through many streets.

Plan the museum visit in its own time block and look for the murals on foot. Check prices and times beforehand.

Tip: Look for the murals on foot.

2. Hidden gems

Cinquantenaire Park💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

Spacious park with triumphal arch and museums. Quieter than the city centre.

🕒 daily about 6 am–10 pm

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Marolles & Place du Jeu de Balle💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

The flea market in the Marolles district is a spot for locals; check opening times and market days beforehand.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Place du Sablon & antiques market💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

The Sablon is a district with the Gothic church of Notre-Dame du Sablon, antique shops and chocolatiers. At weekends there is an antiques market (check days beforehand). The district is quieter than the Grand-Place.

Try pralines from well-known chocolatiers here and stroll through the lanes.

Tip: Come at the weekend for the market.

Mont des Arts & viewpoint💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

The Mont des Arts is an ensemble of park and stairs with a view over the old town and the city’s towers. The view at sunset is especially beautiful. Nearby are museums and the Royal Museums of Fine Arts.

A free break between sights.

Tip: Come at sunset.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Autoworld & museums in the Cinquantenaire

In Cinquantenaire Park stand the triumphal arch and several museums, including the Autoworld museum with vintage cars and the Royal Museum of the Armed Forces. The park suits a walk or a picnic.

Check opening times and prices of the museums beforehand.

Tip: Combine park and museum.

Day trip: Bruges, Ghent or Antwerp

From Brussels you reach Bruges, Ghent and Antwerp by train in about an hour (check timetables beforehand). Bruges has canals and medieval lanes, Ghent a lively old town with a castle, Antwerp the magnificent central station and fashion.

Ghent has its own guide on this site – there you find tips and hidden gems.

Tip: Plan a full day.
