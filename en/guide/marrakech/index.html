My story in Marrakech

I was there in April 2025, and that switch between chaos and calm is what stays. Silence, a pool in the courtyard, tiles in every colour, plants in big pots. By day a dip in the pool, at midday coffee and a shake on a terrace under palms. In the evening a courtyard by candlelight, the pool water glowing, conversations growing quieter. Outside the medina rages – inside you barely notice. My tip: sleep in a riad, not in a hotel block on the outskirts.

Getting there and around

Marrakech has an international airport with taxi and bus links to the medina; agree prices beforehand. Inside the medina you walk.

Marrakech-Menara airport lies about 6 kilometres from the centre; taxis and transfers drive to the medina (negotiate the price beforehand or have your riad pick you up). Cars do not get far in the medina; riad hosts usually collect guests at a gate. Check current entry and currency rules (Moroccan dirham) beforehand.

In the medina you walk; for longer distances take a taxi and agree the price beforehand.

Djemaa el-Fna🆓 Free to experience

Just a few minutes’ walk away: in the evening food stalls, musicians, storytellers, one big bustle. Since 2001 UNESCO intangible heritage – because of the people, not a building. Go to a rooftop terrace above it at sunset.

The Djemaa el-Fna is the main square of the medina and a spectacle in itself: by day juice stalls, snake charmers and henna painters, in the evening dozens of food stalls set up, smoke rises, musicians and storytellers gather audiences. Since 2001 the square has been part of UNESCO’s intangible cultural heritage.

Ask the price at food stalls before ordering and go where locals sit. Money is demanded for photos of snake charmers; clarify this beforehand. Keep bags in view.

Tip: Come at sunset to a rooftop terrace and eat down below afterwards.

Jardin Majorelle

In the morning before it fills up. My tip: book tickets online and come early.

The Jardin Majorelle is a garden with cobalt-blue buildings, cacti, bamboo and palms, laid out by the painter Jacques Majorelle and later saved by Yves Saint Laurent and Pierre Bergé. The famous “Majorelle blue” gives the place its effect. In the garden there is also the Berber Museum.

Buy tickets online and come in the morning at opening, as the garden fills quickly. The neighbouring Yves Saint Laurent Museum is worth its own visit (check prices and times beforehand).

🕒 daily about 8 am–5:30 pm

Tip: Book an online ticket and come at opening.

Bahia Palace

The Bahia Palace dates from the late 19th century and was built for a grand vizier. It consists of courtyards, gardens, painted cedar ceilings, zellige mosaics and stucco-decorated rooms. You walk from courtyard to courtyard and get a feel for Moroccan craftsmanship.

Come early in the morning to avoid groups. Check admission and opening times beforehand. Plan about an hour.

Tip: Come early before the tour groups arrive.

Saadian Tombs

The Saadian Tombs date from the 16th century and were only rediscovered in 1917. The mausoleum room with twelve marble columns and intricate stucco ceilings is impressive; further tombs lie in the garden. The visit is short but worth seeing.

The queue can be long – come early or late. Check prices and times beforehand.

Tip: Come early or shortly before closing.

Koutoubia Mosque🆓 Free to experience

The minaret of the Koutoubia Mosque from the 12th century is Marrakech’s landmark and towers over the medina. Non-Muslims may not enter the mosque, but the gardens and minaret are beautiful from outside, especially in the evening light.

The minaret served as a model for other buildings in the region. Stroll through the gardens in the evening.

Tip: Walk through the gardens in the evening when the light is warm.

Experiencing a hammam

A hammam belongs to Morocco: steam bath, black soap (savon noir), scrub with a glove and a massage. There are simple, traditional hammams for locals and luxurious ones in riads and hotels. Prices differ greatly; clarify beforehand what is included.

Bring swimwear (ask what is customary) and plan about one to two hours. It is a relaxing break from the bustle of the medina.

Tip: Ask about price and procedure beforehand.

Go into the souks without a plan – getting lost is part of it. So is haggling.

Le Jardin Secret & quiet medina gardens💎 Hidden gem

Le Jardin Secret is a restored palace garden in the middle of the medina, with fountains, pavilions and a tower with a view. It is quieter than the better-known gardens and a good break between the souks. There is also a café.

Admission is charged; check price and opening times beforehand.

Tip: Take a break in the garden café.

Menara Gardens💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

The Menara Gardens are a spacious olive grove with a large basin and a pavilion behind which the Atlas Mountains appear in clear weather. They are quiet and suit a walk in the afternoon.

Entry to the grounds is usually cheap; check times beforehand. Come in the late afternoon when the light is soft.

Tip: Come in the late afternoon when the light is soft.

Tanneries & craft alleys💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

In the east of the medina lie tanneries where leather is processed in the traditional way. In the craft alleys you find smiths, dyers, carpet weavers and lamp makers. It smells strongly, and guides offer their services; agree the price beforehand or go without a guide.

A visit gives insight into the crafts that shape the medina. Wear sturdy shoes and take a scarf.

Tip: Agree the price beforehand or go alone.

Day trip: Atlas Mountains, Ourika Valley & Essaouira💎 Hidden gem

The Ourika Valley lies about 60 kilometres from Marrakech and leads into the mountains of the High Atlas: waterfalls, Berber villages and cooler air. Alternatively you drive in about two and a half to three hours to Essaouira, a harbour town on the Atlantic with a white old town and fish market. Both trips are possible by tour or rental car.

Check providers and prices beforehand and plan a full day. Take water, sun protection and a jacket.

Tip: Plan a full day and compare providers.

Tagine, couscous & mint tea

Moroccan cuisine: tagine (stew in a clay pot, for example with lamb and prunes or chicken with lemon and olives), couscous (traditionally on Fridays), pastilla (flaky pastry with pigeon or chicken and almonds) and sweet mint tea. Also try freshly squeezed orange juice on the square.

Ask for prices on menus and eat in places where locals sit. Prices change; ask beforehand.

Tip: Eat where locals sit and ask for prices.

What makes Morocco special

Mint tea, jokingly called “Berber whisky”, souks where haggling is part of it, and a culture where Arabic, Amazigh and French stand side by side. The red clay walls make Marrakech the “Red City”. It was founded in 1070 by the Almoravids; the minaret of the Koutoubia Mosque may still not be overtopped by any building in the medina.

FAQ

Riad or hotel?

Noel’s tip: a riad in the medina.

How many days do you need in Marrakech?
Three days are good; with a trip to the Atlas Mountains or Essaouira rather four.
Is Marrakech safe for tourists?
Mostly yes, but in the medina there are pickpockets and pushy vendors; stay alert and decline offers politely. Check current travel advice beforehand.
