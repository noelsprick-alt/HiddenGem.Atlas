This guide takes you through the must-sees and places locals love, with getting there, prices in Danish kroner (DKK) and a three-day plan. All details are guidelines (2026).

Getting to Copenhagen and around

Arrival

Copenhagen airport (CPH) lies only a few kilometres to the south-east; metro and train get you into the centre in about 15–20 minutes. From Germany you come by train via Hamburg (roughly 4.5–5 hours from Hamburg, including a ferry section or bridge depending on the connection – check current timetables at DB/DSB), by plane or by coach.

In the city

Take a rental bike and drift: the city is flat and has continuous cycle lanes. Metro, S-train and buses also run on a shared zone system.

1. Must-sees

Nyhavn🆓 Free to experience

The colourful harbour dates from the 17th century, and Hans Christian Andersen lived in several of its houses. Most beautiful early in the morning before the terraces fill up.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Church of Our Saviour

Up the spiral tower of the Church of Our Saviour: the outside stair winds around the tower and at the top you have a view over the whole city. Admission is about 125 DKK; not for you if you are afraid of heights or confined spaces.

Tip: The last steps are narrow and slippery in wind. Shortly before sunset it was not crowded.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Torvehallerne🆓 Free to experience

Two market halls with smørrebrød, fresh fish, bakers and flowers. Prices are above average, quality high.

🕒 Mon–Fri 10 am–7 pm, Sat/Sun 10 am–6 pm

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Tivoli Gardens

Tivoli is one of the oldest amusement parks in the world, in the middle of the city, with rides, gardens, concerts and in the evening illumination. Even without rides a walk is worthwhile. Check admission and opening times (seasonal) beforehand.

Come in the evening when the lights come on.

Tip: Come in the evening when the lights come on.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

The Little Mermaid & Kastellet🆓 Free to experience

The Little Mermaid sits on a rock in the harbour and is smaller than many think. On the way lies the star-shaped fortress Kastellet with ramparts and a windmill, ideal for a walk by the water.

Combine both into a morning walk.

Tip: Combine the statue with a walk through Kastellet.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Rundetårn & Rosenborg Castle

The Rundetårn (Round Tower) has a spiral ramp instead of stairs and offers a view over the old town. Rosenborg Castle with its King’s Garden shows crown jewels and treasury (check times and prices beforehand).

Both lie in the city centre and can be combined on foot.

Tip: Combine tower, castle and King’s Garden on foot.

2. Hidden gems & the unusual

Islands Brygge harbour bath💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

If you are brave, jump into the harbour bath: a free harbour bath with a jumping pool, two shallow pools and a 75 m lane. The water is around 20 °C in summer, with occasional jellyfish.

🕒 daily 6 am–10 pm (season) · 💶 free

Tip: Bring a towel; read the notices on site if there are algae or jellyfish.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

CopenHill💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

A waste-to-energy plant with a ski slope on its roof. It is worthwhile even without skis: the lift up is free, with a café and panoramic view at the top.

🕒 Mon noon–5 pm, Tue–Fri noon–7 pm, Sat 10 am–6 pm, Sun 10 am–5 pm

Tip: Take your bike via Nyhavn and the Opera House – a good route.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Reffen – street food by the water🆓 Free to experience

Street food at Reffen in the evening: the largest street-food market in Northern Europe with stalls from around the world, bars and a view of the skyline.

Tip: The place is a bit remote – go by bike or harbour bus, at sunset.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Christiania🆓 Free to experience

In 1971 activists occupied an old military site and declared the Freetown of Christiania – it still exists today. An alternative quarter with workshops, cafés and self-built houses.

Tip: Follow the signs on site, especially regarding photography.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Bakken🆓 Free to experience

North of the city lies Bakken, the oldest amusement park still in operation in the world – admission is free, the rides cost extra.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Day trip: Louisiana Museum & Kronborg Castle💎 Hidden gem

North of Copenhagen lies the Louisiana Museum of Modern Art directly by the sea with a sculpture park. In Helsingør stands Kronborg Castle, known as Hamlet’s castle. You reach both by train (check timetables beforehand).

Plan a full day.

Tip: Plan a full day by train.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Smørrebrød, cinnamon buns & pølser

Try smørrebrød (open rye-bread sandwiches), kanelsnegle (cinnamon buns), pølser (hot dogs) and herring. Danish food is lovingly presented. Prices in Copenhagen are high; ask beforehand.

A hot dog stand is a cheap alternative.

Tip: Try smørrebrød at lunch.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Food & drink

  • Smørrebrød (open sandwiches) at Torvehallerne.
  • Street food at Reffen: many cuisines, in the evening with water views.
  • Bakeries with cinnamon rolls (kanelsnegle) and Danish pastries.

Denmark is expensive – lunch menus and markets save money, tap water is free.

What makes Denmark special

Hygge – the Danish word for cosiness – is a way of life here. Add design on every corner and more bicycles than cars. Bishop Absalon founded the city in the 12th century as a fortress.

My tip: take a rental bike and drift – through Christianshavn, up the spiral tower of the Church of Our Saviour and street food at Reffen in the evening.

FAQ

How many days do you need in Copenhagen?

Three days are ideal, two cover the highlights.

Is Copenhagen expensive?

Yes, above average. Markets, street food and free places like the harbour bath and Bakken admission help save.

Can you really swim in the harbour?

Yes, at designated harbour baths such as Islands Brygge – they are free. Follow notices on site about water quality.
