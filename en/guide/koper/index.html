Koper is Slovenia’s little slice of the Adriatic: a Venetian old town, a harbour promenade and a dip in the sea at the town beach. The country has only about 47 kilometres of coast, which is exactly why Koper, Izola and Piran are so close together that you can see everything in a long weekend.

This guide shows the old town, the prettiest coastal towns and a hike hardly anyone has on their list. Prices and times are guidelines (2026).

Getting to Koper

You reach Koper by train and bus from Ljubljana (around 2 to 2.5 hours, current timetables at Slovenske železnice), by coach from Trieste or Ljubljana and by car on the motorway towards the coast. The nearest airport is Trieste; Ljubljana and Pula are also possible. The old town is small and easy to reach on foot; car parks are on the edge.

Buses run regularly between Koper, Izola and Piran. The distances are short, a rental car is not necessary.

1. The Venetian old town

Titov trg

The heart of the old town: a square with Praetorian Palace, bell tower, loggia and cafés. In December lights and Christmas atmosphere create ambience.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Praetorian Palace

An example of Venetian Gothic (15th century) right on the square. The arches are beautiful, and you can climb onto the balcony.

🕒 daily about 9 am–5 pm

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Cathedral bell tower

The tower next to the cathedral houses a bell from 1333, one of the oldest in Slovenia. The climb costs about €8 and rewards you with a view over the old town and Adriatic.

Town beach & harbour promenade🆓 Free to experience

My tip: a dip in the sea at the town beach and a walk along the harbour promenade when the sun is low.

Cathedral of St Nazarius🆓 Free to experience

The cathedral on Titov trg combines Gothic, Renaissance and Baroque. Next to it stands the bell tower, whose ascent gives you a view over the old town. The church lies directly on the main square and is a good starting point (check opening times beforehand).

Plan 20 to 30 minutes for the church.

Tip: Start here and then climb the tower.

Old-town lanes & Muda Gate🆓 Free to experience

Koper’s old town consists of narrow lanes, small squares and Venetian houses. The Muda Gate is one of the surviving city gates. Away from the main square it becomes quiet, and you find cafés and shops.

Market & Istrian cuisine

At Koper’s market there are fruit, vegetables, cheese and ham from the region. Try fuži (pasta), pršut (air-dried ham), olive oil and the wines Malvazija and Refošk. Prices change; ask beforehand.

Buy olive oil and cheese as souvenirs.

2. Coast & nature: Piran, Izola, Strunjan

Piran – Tartini Square🆓 Free to experience

The prettiest coastal town in Slovenia: Venetian architecture around Tartini Square and St George’s Church with its tower. In early June it is not yet crowded, and restaurant prices are roughly on a par with Croatia.

Tip: Come early in the day; peak season in July and August is much busier.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Izola

A small, calm coastal town between Koper and Piran with pretty old-town lanes, a church with a tower and fewer vendors than elsewhere.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Strunjan Nature Reserve💎 Hidden gem

A coastal path with cliffs, olive groves and salt pans. A well-marked circular route of about 5–7 km with roughly 100 metres of ascent leads to a quiet beach reached only on foot or by boat.

Tip: Park at the salt pans (about €1.80 an hour) and allow 5–6 hours. The path is slippery in rain.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Parenzana cycle path💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

The Parenzana was a narrow-gauge railway that today runs as a cycling and hiking trail through Istria. From Koper you can cycle sections towards Izola and Piran and enjoy tunnels, bridges and sea views. Rental bikes are available in the city.

Check the route and weather beforehand.

Tip: Rent a bike and ride a section.

Sečovlje salt pans💎 Hidden gem

The Sečovlje salt pans near Piran are a nature park where salt is still harvested traditionally. On paths you can watch birds and visit a small museum. A quiet destination near the coast (check times and prices beforehand).

Take binoculars.

Tip: Take binoculars.

Day trip to Trieste

From Koper the Italian port city of Trieste is only about 25 kilometres away. Worth seeing are the large square by the sea, the cafés and Miramare Castle. You can go by bus or car (check timetables and border rules beforehand).

Plan a full day.

Tip: Check timetables and border rules beforehand.

Food & drink

  • Seafood and fish in the coastal towns, often with an Italian touch.
  • Ice cream or coffee at the loggia on Titov trg.
  • Coastal wines such as Malvazija or Refošk.

Prices are similar to Croatia or slightly below.

What makes Slovenia special

More than half the country is forest, and between Alps, karst caves and Adriatic it is often only two hours’ drive. In 1991 Slovenia became independent after a Ten-Day War. More in my guides to Ljubljana and Bled.

FAQ

How many days do you need in Koper?

One day for the old town, two to three with Piran, Izola and Strunjan.

Can you swim in Koper?

Yes, there is a town beach. If you want pebble and rocky coves, go to Strunjan.

Do I need a car?

No, the towns are small and buses connect Koper, Izola and Piran.
