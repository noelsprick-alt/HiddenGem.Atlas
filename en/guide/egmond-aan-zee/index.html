Egmond aan Zee is a small coastal village in North Holland: a long, wide sandy beach, dunes, a lighthouse in the village centre and beach pavilions where you can sit in any weather. It is a place to breathe – without nightlife, but with plenty of nature.

This guide shows what is worth it, where to have breakfast and eat and how to get here without a car. All times and prices are guidelines (2026) – check briefly beforehand, especially off-season.

How to get to Egmond aan Zee

By train and bus

Egmond aan Zee has no station of its own. You take the train to Alkmaar (easy to reach from Amsterdam Centraal) and continue by bus to the coast. Check timetables and the bus line beforehand in a travel app (for example 9292) – they change seasonally.

By car

Alkmaar is about 20 minutes away by car. In high season the small car parks at the beach fill quickly; about 800 m further away you usually still find a spot.

By bike

The area is flat and good for cycling, and the dune area has many paths.

1. Beach, lighthouse & dunes

Egmond aan Zee beach🆓 Free to experience

A long, wide sandy beach: clean, spacious and not overcrowded even in the holiday season – parking is scarcer. The water is often cold for swimming.

The beach is wide and flat, good for walking at low tide, while at high tide the waves come close to the pavilions. Stairs and paths lead down from the promenade; in summer there are lifeguards at supervised sections – pay attention to the flags and do not set up your towel too close to the water.

The water is the North Sea and often fresh even in summer. A windbreak is worth it even when the sun is out. And because Egmond faces west you can watch the sunset directly over the sea.

Tip: Check the tides – the beach is widest at low tide, tight at high tide.

J.C.J. van Speijk lighthouse

The 37-metre lighthouse stands right in the village, close to the beach. From the top you have a wide view over the village, dunes and North Sea.

The lighthouse commemorates the Dutch naval officer Jan van Speijk, who in 1831 blew up his ship off Antwerp so it would not fall into enemy hands. It has stood in the village since the 19th century and is the landmark of Egmond aan Zee.

Even without climbing it the spot at the foot of the tower is worth it: a promenade leads from here to the beach, and the tower works well as an orientation point. If you want to go up, check the opening times beforehand – outside the season it is usually only accessible by arrangement.

🕒 usually open in July and August, otherwise groups by appointment only – check times beforehand

Tip: Check opening times beforehand – the door is not always open.

Lookout tower (Uitkijktoren) in the woods💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

A small lookout tower reached by two woodland paths with log-and-sand steps in about 3 minutes. An insider tip for sunset with a view over the whole village.

The steps are steep and sandy, and a small platform waits at the top. At sunset the tower is a quiet place: you look over forest, dunes and sea without anyone bumping into you. Bring a jacket – the wind whistles at the top.

Tip: Come 30–45 minutes before sunset, then you have time to climb up and down in daylight.

North Holland Dune Reserve

One of the largest nature areas in the Netherlands – for walking, cycling and birdwatching. Highland cattle and wild horses live here.

The reserve stretches for many kilometres along the coast. There are marked hiking trails, cycle routes and viewpoints; with luck you see deer, foxes or birds of prey. Stay on the paths, because the dunes are fragile, and keep dogs on a lead where required.

If you want to experience the dunes without crowds, set off in the morning or evening. Take water and snacks, because there is often nothing to buy along the way.

By bike through the dunes (junction network)💎 Hidden gem

In the Netherlands cycle routes are signposted with numbered junctions: you simply follow the number of the next point and put your route together yourself. Around Egmond many of these routes run through dunes and woods and are mostly flat – manageable even without great fitness.

A classic loop leads from the village through the dune reserve to Bergen and back via Egmond-Binnen. You usually find a map on notice boards and at info points; along the way there are cafés to stop at. You can rent bikes in the village – ask about prices and availability beforehand.

With a headwind the ride back by the sea is tiring: it is better to start into the wind, then you have it at your back on the way home.

Tip: Start into the wind and plan a coffee break in Bergen.

Surfing, kiting & beach sports

The wide beach and steady wind make Egmond a place for water sports. Surf and kitesurf schools offer courses and rental depending on the season; stand-up paddling and beach running are also popular. Beginners should book courses and pay attention to notes about current and wind – the North Sea is rougher than a lake.

The water is often fresh even in summer; a wetsuit makes sense if you want to be in the water for long. If you prefer watching, sit in one of the beach pavilions and watch the kiters jump.

Tip: Ask the school about the wind forecast and water temperature – and whether a wetsuit is included in the price.

Egmond in winter – storm, waves & half marathon💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

Egmond is not just a summer destination: in winter the beach is almost empty, the waves are higher, and a walk into the wind feels like a holiday. Egmond can also be experienced particularly calmly in winter: many cafés are open, but you share them with fewer people.

The village is also known for the Egmond Half Marathon, traditionally held in January along the beach and through the dunes, attracting many runners. If you travel then, book accommodation early – rooms are scarce (check date and course beforehand).

Tip: Pack a windbreaker, hat and gloves – on the beach it is much colder in winter than in the village.

2. Abbey, museums & history

Egmond Abbey (Egmond-Binnen)💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

St Adelbert’s Abbey in the neighbouring village of Egmond-Binnen is the village’s main attraction. Benedictine monks still live and work here. There is a history museum, a gallery shop, a meditation centre and a butterfly garden. Stillness, gardens and a chapel with special acoustics.

The abbey has a long history: a first monastery on this spot is considered a foundation of the Counts of Holland in the early Middle Ages; later it was destroyed and rebuilt as a Benedictine abbey in the 20th century. Today monks live there again, praying, working and running a shop with products.

Visitors can see the garden and church and attend services. Plan about one to two hours and dress quietly – it is a place of silence, not an amusement park. By bike the abbey is easy to reach from the beach.

🕒 Tue–Sat 11 am–4:30 pm, closed Sun/Mon

Tip: Take your time – it is a place to settle, not to tick off.

Museum Prins Hendrik de Zeevaarder

A small free museum in a former home for retired fishermen on Voorstraat with hand-built sailing ship models and stained-glass windows that tell a sailor’s life. A small donation is welcome.

The museum shows how fishermen and sailors used to live – with models, window pictures and personal objects. It is small and free to enter, a good destination for a rain shower. If you have time, talk to the volunteers: they often tell you more about the place than is on the signs.

Bunker museum & castle ruin

In the dunes near the North Boulevard a bunker museum recalls Egmond in the Second World War. In Egmond aan den Hoef you find the remains of the castle in a lake and the castle chapel founded in 1227.

In the Second World War the coast was part of the Atlantic Wall; many bunkers still stand in the dunes, and some are museums today. The bunker museum tells how the occupation changed the village – if you want to understand the history of the North Sea coast you find an introduction here. Opening times are often seasonal, so check beforehand.

Tip: Opening hours are on the respective museum websites – they vary by season.

Beach pavilions: Uitkijk, Evi Beach, Bad Egmond, De Zilvermeeuw

Several beach pavilions sit right on the sea. At Uitkijk you sit behind a big window front with a sea view and eat fish, at Evi Beach the stove burns in winter. Prices are typical for a beach location.

Tip: A pavilion is worth it in winter too: some are heated and enclosed so you can look at the sea dry in the wind.

Dutch beach food: kibbeling, herring & poffertjes

A day at the North Sea beach includes a typical snack programme: kibbeling (fried pieces of fish, usually with herb sauce), herring (traditionally raw with onions), chips with mayo and sweet poffertjes (small pancakes with icing sugar). Many beach pavilions and snack stands have these classics on the menu.

If you want to try: ask for “kibbeling” at the snack stand, then find a bench with a view of the sea and eat while it is warm. Prices change, but for snack stands expect roughly double-digit euro amounts for a meal per person – ask for the price when ordering.

Tip: Try kibbeling first – and share a portion of poffertjes, they are sweet and filling.

4. Day trips

Alkmaar

Known for the cheese market held on Fridays from April to October, plus several museums (including a cheese museum and a beer museum). Alkmaar is also your transfer point – ideal for combining.

The cheese market takes place in season on Friday morning at the Waagplein: traders carry cheese wheels on wooden barrows, cheese carriers in white clothing weigh and sell them. It is touristy, but a typical Dutch spectacle. Come early, otherwise you stand in the second row.

Away from the market a walk along the canals and through the old town is worth it. If you have time, visit the cheese museum or the beer museum. Please check the dates and times of the cheese market beforehand.

Tip: Arrive before 10 a.m. for the cheese market, otherwise you stand behind rows of people.

Haarlem and Amsterdam

Haarlem has canals, a historic old town and the Frans Hals Museum. Amsterdam is also doable as a day trip.

Haarlem lies between the dunes and Amsterdam and has a pretty old town with the market square Grote Markt and the Gothic St Bavo Church. The Frans Hals Museum shows painting of the “Golden Age”, plus small shops, cafés and hofjes (almshouse courtyards) with hidden gardens.

Amsterdam is possible from Egmond as a long day trip; if you want to see a lot there, it is better to stay overnight in Amsterdam. Plan the connections in advance.

Zee Aquarium in Bergen aan Zee

Over 300 fish species, shark and piranha displays and a touch pool with rays – good in rain or with children.

The aquarium shows marine animals from the North Sea and from distant seas; these include sharks, rays and crabs. It is a good rainy-day option, especially with children. Plan about one to two hours and check prices and opening times beforehand.

Schoorl Dunes & Hoge Berg🆓 Free to experience

North of Bergen lie the Schoorl Dunes, one of the highest dune landscapes in the Netherlands. The “Hoge Berg” at a good 50 metres is a popular target because from the top you look over dunes, forest and sea. The paths are marked; hiking in sand is harder on the legs than it looks.

If you come by bike you can leave bikes at the entrances and continue on foot. Take water, sun protection in sun and a jacket in wind. Getting there from Egmond is by bike, car or by bus with a change – check timetables beforehand.

Tip: Go early or in the late afternoon – the light is nicer and it is quieter.

Bergen & Bergen aan Zee🆓 Free to experience

Bergen is a green village between dunes and forest that has long attracted artists; a “Bergen School” shaped Dutch painting in the 20th century. In the village you find galleries, cafés and the Museum Kranenburgh, which shows regional art (check times beforehand).

Bergen aan Zee, by the sea, is smaller and quieter than Egmond: a beach, a few pavilions and the Zee Aquarium. If you find Egmond too lively you can plan another beach day here.

Tip: Combine the visit with a bike ride through the dune reserve.

Where to stay

FAQ

How many days do you need in Egmond aan Zee?

One day covers beach, lighthouse and abbey; a weekend is ideal for relaxing and trips to Alkmaar or Haarlem.

Can you swim in Egmond aan Zee?

In high summer yes – in July/August the water is around 20 °C, outside peak season it is too cold for most people.

How do I get to Egmond aan Zee without a car?

By train to Alkmaar, then on by bus. Check current connections in a travel app.

Is Egmond aan Zee good for families?

Yes: plenty of beach space, dunes to explore and an aquarium nearby. The area is very family-friendly.

What can I do in bad weather?

A heated beach pavilion, the abbey and its museum, the free Prins Hendrik museum or the Zee Aquarium in Bergen aan Zee.

Can you explore Egmond aan Zee by bike?
Yes – a dense network of cycle routes with numbered junctions leads through dunes and woods to Bergen or Alkmaar. You can rent bikes in the village; allow for headwind by the sea.
Where is the best sunset in Egmond?
Egmond is on the west coast, so you see the sun set directly into the sea – on the beach, at a pavilion or from the small lookout tower in the woods.
