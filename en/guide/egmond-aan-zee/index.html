Egmond aan Zee is a small coastal village in North Holland: a long, wide sandy beach, dunes, a lighthouse in the village centre and beach pavilions where you can sit in any weather. It is a place to breathe – according to visitors without nightlife, but with plenty of nature.

This guide shows what is worth it, where to have breakfast and eat and how to get here without a car. All times and prices are guidelines (2026) – check briefly beforehand, especially off-season.

How to get to Egmond aan Zee

By train and bus

Egmond aan Zee has no station of its own. You take the train to Alkmaar (easy to reach from Amsterdam Centraal) and continue by bus to the coast. Check timetables and the bus line beforehand in a travel app (for example 9292) – they change seasonally.

By car

Alkmaar is about 20 minutes away by car. In high season the small car parks at the beach fill quickly; one visitor parked about 800 m away and was happy with that.

By bike

The area is flat and good for cycling – according to travel portals the dune area has many paths.

1. Beach, lighthouse & dunes

Egmond aan Zee beach

A long, wide sandy beach with 4.6 stars on Google from around 4,000 reviews. Visitors describe it as clean, spacious and not overcrowded even in the holiday season – parking is scarcer. The water is often cold for swimming.

Tip: Dogs are only allowed on the beach in certain months according to visitors – check the current rules first. A sunbed plus parasol cost about €17 a day at one pavilion according to a review.

📍 View in Google Maps

J.C.J. van Speijk lighthouse

The 37-metre lighthouse stands right in the village, close to the beach. From the top you have a wide view over the village, dunes and North Sea. On Google it has 4.6 stars.

🕒 according to the village portal open in July and August, otherwise groups by appointment only; Google lists different times

Tip: Check opening beforehand – several visitors found it closed.

📍 View in Google Maps

Lookout tower (Uitkijktoren) in the woods💎 Hidden gem

A small lookout tower reached by two woodland paths with log-and-sand steps in about 3 minutes. Visitors call it an insider tip for sunset with a view over the whole village. 4.3 stars on Google.

Tip: The climb is steep and not suitable for strollers or wheelchairs. Bring a jacket for the wind.

📍 View in Google Maps

North Holland Dune Reserve

One of the largest nature areas in the Netherlands – for walking, cycling and birdwatching. According to travel portals Highland cattle and wild horses live here.

2. Abbey, museums & history

Egmond Abbey (Egmond-Binnen)💎 Hidden gem

St Adelbert’s Abbey in the neighbouring village of Egmond-Binnen is the village’s main attraction. Benedictine monks still live and work here. There is a history museum, a gallery shop, a meditation centre and a butterfly garden. Visitors praise the stillness, gardens and the chapel’s acoustics. 4.6 stars on Google.

🕒 Tue–Sat 11 am–4:30 pm according to Google, closed Sun/Mon

Tip: Take your time – it is a place to settle, not to tick off.

📍 View in Google Maps

Museum Prins Hendrik de Zeevaarder

A small free museum in a former home for retired fishermen on Voorstraat with hand-built sailing ship models and stained-glass windows that tell a sailor’s life. A small donation is welcome.

📍 View in Google Maps

Bunker museum & castle ruin

In the dunes near the North Boulevard a bunker museum recalls Egmond in the Second World War. In Egmond aan den Hoef you find the remains of the castle in a lake and the castle chapel founded in 1227.

Tip: Opening hours are on the respective museum websites – they vary by season.

3. Breakfast, beach pavilions & cafés

Toast Egmond💎 Hidden gem

Breakfast café on Voorstraat. On Google it has 4.9 stars from around 330 reviews; guests praise the breakfast, the friendly owner and the poffertjes.

🕒 daily 8:30 am–5 pm according to Google, closed Wednesdays

📍 View in Google Maps

Pompadour Ontbijt- & Lunchroom

Cosy breakfast and lunch café on Voorstraat with homemade apple pie. Visitors suggest ordering a coffee and sharing a dish because portions are big. 4.6 stars on Google.

🕒 daily 8 am–5 pm according to Google, closed Tuesdays

📍 View in Google Maps

Beach pavilions: Uitkijk, Evi Beach, Bad Egmond, De Zilvermeeuw

Several beach pavilions sit right on the sea – all with around 4.3–4.4 stars on Google. At Uitkijk guests praise the view through the big window front and the fish dishes, at Evi Beach the stove in winter. Prices are typical for a beach location.

Tip: A pavilion is worth it in winter too: some are heated and enclosed so you can look at the sea dry in the wind.

📍 View in Google Maps

Het Wapen van Egmond

Grand café on Voorstraat with a terrace and a German menu. 4.5 stars on Google from over 2,000 reviews; guests recommend reserving beforehand.

🕒 closed Thursdays according to Google

📍 View in Google Maps

Piet Kerssens – real baker

A bakery on Voorstraat open daily from about 7:30 am – good for rolls and pastry for the beach day.

4. Day trips

Alkmaar

Known for the cheese market held on Fridays from April to October, plus several museums (including a cheese museum and a beer museum). Alkmaar is also your transfer point – ideal for combining.

📍 View in Google Maps

Haarlem and Amsterdam

Haarlem has canals, a historic old town and the Frans Hals Museum. Amsterdam is also doable as a day trip.

📍 View in Google Maps

Zee Aquarium in Bergen aan Zee

Over 300 fish species, shark and piranha displays and a touch pool with rays – good in rain or with children.

Where to stay

  • Near the beach boulevard: short ways to beach and pavilions, lively in summer.
  • Voorstraat and village centre: cafés, bakers and shops on foot.
  • Holiday flats and houses: the choice is big, including bungalows and villas.

Tip: book early for summer holidays. Off-season many places are quieter and cheaper.

Your plan

Day 1: One day in Egmond aan Zee

  1. Morning – breakfast at Toast Egmond or Pompadour, rolls from the baker for later
  2. Late morning – see the lighthouse (check opening) and a beach walk
  3. Midday – fish or a sandwich at a beach pavilion
  4. Afternoon – abbey in Egmond-Binnen (Tue–Sat) or the dune reserve
  5. Evening – lookout tower at sunset, dinner in the village (much closes around 10–11 pm)

FAQ

How many days do you need in Egmond aan Zee?

One day covers beach, lighthouse and abbey; a weekend is ideal for relaxing and trips to Alkmaar or Haarlem.

Can you swim in Egmond aan Zee?

In high summer yes – travel portals quote water temperatures around 20 °C in July/August, while visitors often find the water too cold outside peak season.

How do I get to Egmond aan Zee without a car?

By train to Alkmaar, then on by bus. Check current connections in a travel app.

Is Egmond aan Zee good for families?

Yes: plenty of beach space, dunes to explore and an aquarium nearby. Travel portals describe the area as family-friendly.

What can I do in bad weather?

A heated beach pavilion, the abbey and its museum, the free Prins Hendrik museum or the Zee Aquarium in Bergen aan Zee.
