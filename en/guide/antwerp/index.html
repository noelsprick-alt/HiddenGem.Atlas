Getting to Antwerp

You reach Antwerp from NRW by train (e.g. via Brussels or the Thalys/Eurostar network) or coach; check current connections beforehand. The old town is compact, you walk most of it.

Antwerp is easy to reach by train from Brussels, Ghent, Amsterdam and Cologne; the central station is in the middle of the city. The old town can be explored on foot; for Zurenborg and Het Zuid you take tram or bus.

Check timetables beforehand.

1. Must-sees

Antwerpen-Centraal

The “railway cathedral”: high dome, wide staircases, palace feeling – worth seeing even if you are not taking a train.

Antwerpen-Centraal station opened in 1905 and is considered one of the most beautiful stations in the world because of its dome, stairs and iron arches. The main hall is high and bright; several levels lead to the platforms. Even if you are not travelling it is worth a look inside.

Plan a quarter of an hour to see and photograph the hall and the stairs.

Tip: Go up the grand staircase and photograph from above.

MAS – Museum aan de Stroom🆓 Free to experience

The museum puts Antwerp’s port history in a red tower. The rooftop is free and has a 360° view, especially at sunset.

🕒 Tue–Sun 10 am–5 pm, closed Mondays

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Grote Markt & town hall🆓 Free to experience

The Grote Markt is the main square with guild houses and the Renaissance town hall. In the middle stands the Brabo Fountain: the hero Brabo throws a severed hand into the Scheldt – so the legend of the name’s origin goes. Cafés and terraces invite you to sit.

Visit the square in the morning or early evening.

Tip: Come in the morning or early evening.

Cathedral of Our Lady

The Gothic cathedral with its high tower is the city’s landmark. Inside hang paintings by Peter Paul Rubens, including the Descent from the Cross. The visit is charged (check prices and times beforehand).

Plan about an hour.

Tip: See Rubens’ Descent from the Cross.

Rubens House

The Rubens House was the home and studio of Peter Paul Rubens. You see living rooms, the courtyard with garden and paintings by the artist. The house conveys how a 17th-century painter lived and worked.

Check admission and times beforehand.

Tip: Plan about an hour.

Diamond district & fashion district🆓 Free to experience

Antwerp is a centre of the diamond trade; around the central station lies the diamond district with jewellers. In the fashion district around Nationalestraat you find boutiques and designers. The area is a window into fashion and trade history.

Stroll through the streets and look at the shop windows.

Tip: Stroll through the streets in the afternoon.

2. Hidden gems

Cogels-Osylei💎 Hidden gem

The Art Nouveau façades in the Zurenborg quarter: street after street of magnificent houses. You reach it easily from Berchem or Centraal, with cafés waiting at either end of the street.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Vlaeykensgang💎 Hidden gem

A hidden historic alley in the heart of the old town, quiet and photogenic.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

A bolleke at De Koninck

In the evening a bolleke, the city’s amber beer. The city brewery offers an interactive tour with tasting.

🕒 Tue/Wed 11 am–7 pm, Thu–Sun 11 am–10 pm, closed Mondays

Tip: Book the tour online.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Het Zuid & art quarter💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

In the south of the city lies Het Zuid, a district with museums such as the Royal Museum of Fine Arts (KMSKA), galleries, cafés and restaurants. It feels more relaxed than the old town.

Combine a museum visit and a coffee break.

Tip: Combine museum and coffee break.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Scheldt riverbank & Het Steen💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

On the river lies the medieval castle Het Steen, and on the promenade you can stroll along the water. From here you see cargo ships and the city skyline. It is especially beautiful in the evening.

Plan a walk by the water.

Tip: Come for an evening walk.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Belgian cuisine: fries, waffles & Antwerpse handjes

Try fries with sauce, waffles, pralines and the Antwerpse handjes, small shortbread hands that recall the Brabo legend. A beer like the Bolleke from De Koninck goes with it.

There are many places in the old town; ask for prices.

Tip: Try the biscuit hands as a souvenir.
