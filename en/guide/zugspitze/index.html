Getting there and around

You reach Garmisch-Partenkirchen by train from Munich. Zugspitze railway and Eibsee start from Garmisch; check prices and times beforehand.

You reach Garmisch-Partenkirchen from Munich by regional train in about 1.5 hours (check timetables beforehand). From Garmisch station the cog railway runs directly to the Zugspitze; buses and trains go to the Eibsee, and there is the Eibsee cable car to the summit. A guest card from your accommodation often brings discounts for bus and rail.

By car you come via the A95 from Munich; parking in Garmisch and at the Eibsee is charged. In good weather in high season expect long waits at the railways and book tickets online.

1. Must-sees

Zugspitze – Zahnradbahn hoch, Seilbahn runter

At 2,962 metres Germany’s highest mountain.

The cog railway starts at Garmisch-Partenkirchen station and takes you through forest and a long tunnel section to the Zugspitzplatt, a high plateau at about 2,600 metres with glacier remnants and a ski area. From there a glacier cable car goes to the summit. The railway is an experience in itself: you rise from the town into the mountains without needing a car.

At the summit await a viewing platform, restaurants, the Münchner Haus and the golden summit cross. On clear days you see four countries – Germany, Austria, Italy and Switzerland. Ticket prices are high (in the higher double-digit euro range; check beforehand, there are often online discounts). Bring a warm jacket – it is cool at the top even in summer.

Tip: Go up early in the morning, then the view is best and the cable cars emptier.

Eibsee-Runde🆓 Free to experience

A loop around the lake.

The Eibsee lies at the foot of the Zugspitze and is one of the most beautiful lakes in Bavaria: turquoise-green water, small islands and forest on the shore. The circular path is about seven to eight kilometres long, runs over boardwalks and through the forest, and takes about two hours. You can also swim in the lake when the temperature allows, or rent a boat.

Parking at the lake is limited and charged; by train or bus from Garmisch you arrive easily. Start early in the morning – then the water is mirror-smooth and the path is empty.

Tip: Walk the loop before or after the trip to the summit – with a time buffer.

Summit: view, glacier & summit cross

At the summit you can cross a small bridge onto Austrian territory – a border you barely notice in the middle of the mountains. From the viewing platform you look over dozens of peaks, valleys and lakes. On the Zugspitzplatt you can ski in winter or walk a bit across snowfields in summer.

The weather changes quickly up there. Check the webcams before you go – in fog you see little. If you do not want to be caught out by clouds, plan a second day as a reserve.

Tip: Check the webcams before leaving – clear visibility is everything.

Garmisch-Partenkirchen – Ludwigstraße & Lüftlmalerei🆓 Free to experience

Garmisch-Partenkirchen consists of two former villages. Partenkirchen is the older part with Ludwigstraße, where painted façades (Lüftlmalerei), small shops and cafés shape the picture. Here you feel as if you are in the middle of a Bavarian mountain village.

On a walk through the streets you see fountains, chapels and gingerbread shops. Plan an hour and eat something Bavarian – Kaiserschmarrn or cheese spätzle.

Tip: Use the town as a base – the railway starts here.

Olympic ski stadium & large Olympic ski jump

The Große Olympiaschanze (large Olympic ski jump) is a landmark of Garmisch: every year at the turn of the year the Four Hills Tournament takes place here. You can visit the site and imagine how the jumpers feel. The stadium below hosted the 1936 Winter Olympics.

If you go to the Partnach Gorge you pass by here. In summer the site is quiet, in January it is very crowded – secure tickets early.

Tip: Combine the visit with the Partnach Gorge.

2. Hidden gems

Partnachklamm💎 Hidden gem

Come early and you have the gorge almost to yourself. Admission is about €10, and it is a 20–30-minute walk from the car park.

The Partnach Gorge is a gorge about 700 metres long whose rock walls are up to 80 metres high. The path leads over walkways and through tunnels right beside the roaring water – refreshing in summer, with icicles and frozen waterfalls in winter. Admission is low (check prices beforehand), the route short.

You reach the gorge from the Olympic ski stadium in Garmisch in about 20 to 30 minutes on foot. Wear non-slip shoes and a jacket – it is wet and cool. In high water or avalanche danger the gorge may be closed.

Tip: Come early, bring a jacket and check beforehand whether the gorge is open.

Badersee – the quiet moor lake💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

The Badersee near Grainau is a small moor lake whose water becomes surprisingly warm in summer. Around it lie meadows, woods and a view of the Wetterstein mountains. There is a small bathing area; you can swim, lie in the sun and stop at the lakeside restaurant.

Compared with the Eibsee it is quieter and more family-friendly. On hot weekends it gets crowded – then come early. A good tip for anyone who still wants to get in the water after the mountain tour.

Tip: Come early or on a weekday – on hot weekends it is crowded.

Höllentalklamm – wilder than the Partnach Gorge💎 Hidden gem

The Höllentalklamm lies in the Höllental valley, at the foot of the Zugspitze massif. The path leads over bridges and through rock tunnels along a wild waterfall. The gorge is open only in the summer half-year; access is via the Höllentalklamm hut. Expect several hours of hiking (check times and prices beforehand).

If you like mountain hiking the gorge is a spectacular destination and less crowded than the Partnach Gorge. Good shoes and sure-footedness are a must.

Tip: Open only in summer – check weather and access beforehand.

Kramer Plateau Trail💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

The Kramer Plateau Trail is a hike around the Kramer, a mountain above Garmisch. The path leads through forest, over meadows and offers repeated views of the Zugspitze and the Wetterstein mountains. It is not extremely strenuous but long – plan half a day.

Along the way there are huts where you can stop. Take sturdy shoes, water and something to eat. A good plan B when the Zugspitze is hidden in cloud.

Tip: A good weather plan B when the summit is in cloud.

Linderhof Palace & Neuschwanstein as a day trip

From Garmisch you reach the royal castles in about an hour’s drive: Linderhof Palace, Ludwig II’s only completed building, and Neuschwanstein Castle, the fairytale castle. Both are popular excursion destinations; tickets should be reserved online in good time.

Linderhof is smaller, quieter and surrounded by a magnificent park. Neuschwanstein is overcrowded but impressive. If you only have half a day, choose Linderhof.

Tip: Reserve tickets online and start early.

FAQ

How do I get up the Zugspitze?

By cog railway and cable car.

When is the best time for the Zugspitze?
For hiking and clear views June to October, for skiing December to April. Always check the webcams.
Do I need winter gear?
There is often snow at the top, even in summer. A warm jacket, hat, sturdy shoes and sun protection make sense.
