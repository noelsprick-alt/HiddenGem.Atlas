My story in Seoul

After Japan, Seoul was my next stop – and the contrast could hardly have been bigger. Where Tokyo is quiet, Seoul is loud, fast and hungry. My tip for a day: Bukchon Hanok Village in the morning, the food stalls of Gwangjang Market for lunch and up Namsan to N Seoul Tower at night when the city starts to glow.

Getting to Seoul and around

Seoul is served by Incheon airport; express train or bus takes you into the city. In the city the subway is the backbone: fast, clean, signed in English too.

1. Must-sees

Bukchon Hanok Village

Through the old wooden houses in the morning. 4.4 stars on Google. Visitors advise coming early and keeping quiet – it is a residential area; Google lists visiting hours (about 10 am–5 pm).

📍 View in Google Maps

Gwangjang Market

For lunch to the food stalls: mung bean pancakes (bindaetteok) on stools. 4.2 stars on Google from over 44,000 reviews – touristy but worthwhile.

📍 View in Google Maps

N Seoul Tower on Namsan

Up Namsan in the evening when the city starts to glow. 4.5 stars on Google. Visitors skip the cable car and walk up.

🕒 daily about 10 am–10:30 pm according to Google

📍 View in Google Maps

2. Crazy but true

Third Tunnel at the border💎 Hidden gem

At the border with North Korea you can walk into a tunnel with a helmet that North Korea secretly dug under the border – discovered in 1978. 4.6 stars on Google. Visitors report a steep descent and low ceiling; photography is forbidden inside the tunnel.

🕒 Tue–Sun about 9 am–2:40 pm, closed Mondays according to Google

Tip: Usually only as part of a DMZ tour; book in advance.

📍 View in Google Maps

Noryangjin fish market

Here you pick your food alive from the tank. 3.9 stars on Google: visitors like the freshness but warn about unclear cooking fees – agree prices beforehand.

📍 View in Google Maps

What makes South Korea special

Seoul has been the capital since 1394, then of the Joseon dynasty – Gyeongbokgung Palace dates from that time. King Sejong had the Hangeul alphabet developed in 1443 so that ordinary people could read too. After the Korean War the city lay in ruins; the rapid recovery is still called the “Miracle on the Han River”.
