My story in Seoul

After Japan, Seoul was my next stop – and the contrast could hardly have been bigger. Where Tokyo is quiet, Seoul is loud, fast and hungry. My tip for a day: Bukchon Hanok Village in the morning, the food stalls of Gwangjang Market for lunch and up Namsan to N Seoul Tower at night when the city starts to glow.

Getting to Seoul and around

Seoul is served by Incheon airport; express train or bus takes you into the city. In the city the subway is the backbone: fast, clean, signed in English too.

Incheon airport is about an hour from the centre; AREX train and airport buses go into the city (check timetables beforehand). In Seoul you use the metro with a T-money card; the lines are well signposted, also in English. The currency is the won.

Navigation apps like Naver Map or KakaoMap work better than some Western apps.

1. Must-sees

Bukchon Hanok Village🆓 Free to experience

Through the old wooden houses in the morning. Come early and keep quiet – it is a residential area; visiting hours apply (about 10 am–5 pm).

Bukchon Hanok Village lies between the palaces Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung and consists of traditional wooden houses (hanok) with curved tiled roofs. The district is a residential area; visitors should be quiet and follow house rules. In some hanok you can drink tea or see crafts.

Come early in the morning. You get the best view in the lanes with a view over the city.

Tip: Come early and be quiet – it is a residential area.

Up Namsan in the evening when the city starts to glow. My tip: skip the cable car and walk up.

Gyeongbokgung Palace

Gyeongbokgung is the largest palace of the Joseon dynasty with throne hall, pavilions and gardens. The changing of the guard at the gate is a highlight. If you wear hanbok (traditional clothing) admission is often free or reduced (check rules beforehand).

Plan two hours. Tuesday is often a rest day (check beforehand).

Tip: Come for the changing of the guard and plan two hours.

Myeongdong & cosmetics

Myeongdong is a shopping district with cosmetics shops, fashion and street-food stalls. In the evening the street fills with stalls. Korean cosmetics are popular; many shops give samples.

Compare prices and note tax refunds for tourists (check rules beforehand).

Tip: Come in the evening for street food.

Hongdae – student district & live music🆓 Free to experience

Hongdae lies around Hongik University and is known for street music, clubs, cafés, street art and fashion. In the evening and at weekends it is especially lively. You can watch street performers or try karaoke.

Come in the evening.

Tip: Come in the evening and try karaoke (noraebang).

Insadong & teahouses

Insadong is a street with teahouses, crafts, galleries and traditional shops. Here you can drink tea and buy souvenirs. The side lanes hide small cafés and courtyards.

Try Korean teas and sweets like hotteok.

Tip: Drink tea in a traditional teahouse.

2. Crazy but true

Third Tunnel at the border💎 Hidden gem

At the border with North Korea you can walk into a tunnel with a helmet that North Korea secretly dug under the border – discovered in 1978. The descent is steep and the ceiling low; photography is forbidden inside the tunnel.

🕒 Tue–Sun about 9 am–2:40 pm, closed Mondays

Tip: Usually only as part of a DMZ tour; book in advance.

Here you pick your food alive from the tank. Super fresh – but agree the cooking fee beforehand.

Ihwa Mural Village & Naksan Park💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

Ihwa Mural Village is a district with murals on the hill by the city wall; Naksan Park offers a walk along the old wall with a view of the city, especially in the evening. The district is residential; be considerate.

Go in the late afternoon and stay until sunset.

Tip: Go at sunset and be considerate.

Cheonggyecheon stream💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

The Cheonggyecheon is a restored stream flowing through the city centre. Along the water run paths, bridges and seating steps. In the evening it is lit. A calm walk in the middle of the city.

Walk a section in the evening.

Tip: Go in the evening when the stream is lit.

Korean cuisine: kimchi, bibimbap & BBQ

Korean food is an experience: kimchi as a side, bibimbap (rice with vegetables and egg), bulgogi, Korean BBQ to grill yourself, tteokbokki and hotteok. Many dishes are ordered for two. With BBQ you grill at the table.

Prices change; ask beforehand. Many places have picture menus.

Tip: Order for two and have them show you how to grill.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Day trip: Suwon Fortress or Nami Island

Hwaseong Fortress in Suwon (UNESCO World Heritage) lies about an hour from Seoul and is a lovely day trip with a walk along the walls. Nami Island offers avenues and nature, especially in autumn. Both destinations are reachable by train or bus (check timetables beforehand).

Plan a full day.

Tip: Plan a full day.

What makes South Korea special

Seoul has been the capital since 1394, then of the Joseon dynasty – Gyeongbokgung Palace dates from that time. King Sejong had the Hangeul alphabet developed in 1443 so that ordinary people could read too. After the Korean War the city lay in ruins; the rapid recovery is still called the “Miracle on the Han River”.
