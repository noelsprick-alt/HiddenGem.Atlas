Getting there and around

Hanoi is served by Noi Bai airport. Between cities flying is best – domestic flights are cheap.

Noi Bai airport lies about 30 to 45 minutes from the Old Quarter; taxi, Grab and airport buses go to the centre (check prices beforehand). In the city you use Grab, taxi or walk. Cash in dong is practical; ATMs are everywhere.

Tip: download the Grab app before arrival so you do not have to haggle over prices.

1. Must-sees

Hoan Kiem Lake in the morning🆓 Free to experience

Out early when people do tai chi.

Hoan Kiem Lake lies in the middle of Hanoi and is the heart of the city. Early in the morning locals jog, dance and do tai chi on the shore, later strollers and couples arrive. On a small island stands the Turtle Tower, and the red wooden Huc Bridge leads to the Ngoc Son Temple on an island in the lake.

At weekends streets around the lake are partly car-free, then it is especially lively (check times beforehand). A lap around the lake takes about 30 to 40 minutes.

Tip: Be there at 6 a.m. and walk a lap around the lake.

The Old Quarter’s 36 streets🆓 Free to experience

Hanoi’s Old Quarter consists of narrow streets originally named after crafts and goods: silver, silk, bamboo, copper. Today shops, cafés, street kitchens and hostels line up, with mopeds pushing between them. It is loud, colourful and chaotic – and that is exactly the appeal.

Cross streets slowly and steadily, then the mopeds will dodge you. Plan at least half a day to drift.

Tip: Let yourself drift and cross streets slowly.

Temple of Literature (Văn Miếu)

The Temple of Literature dates from the 11th century and was Vietnam’s first university. Five courtyards, gateways and stone stelae on turtles listing the names of graduates. Despite the visitors, the courtyard has a calm mood.

Come early in the morning or in the afternoon to avoid groups. Check admission and times beforehand.

Tip: Come early in the morning.

Hỏa Lò Prison

Hỏa Lò Prison was built by the French in the 19th century and later called the “Hanoi Hilton” by Americans. The museum shows cells, exhibits and stories from colonial times and war. It is an oppressive but instructive place.

Plan one to two hours. Check prices and opening times beforehand.

Tip: Plan one to two hours.

Water puppet theatre

Water puppet theatre is a Vietnamese tradition: puppets dance on water, accompanied by live music and singing. Performances last about an hour and tell stories of village life, fishermen and legends. It is touristy but charming.

Better book tickets in advance; check times and prices beforehand.

Tip: Book tickets in advance.

Train Street💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

In a narrow lane in Hanoi the train passes very close to houses and cafés. This is spectacular but also dangerous; rules and access change, and at times the lane is closed to visitors. Find out the current situation beforehand.

Always keep a distance from the tracks and follow instructions on site. Safety before photo.

Tip: Check the current situation beforehand and keep your distance.

Dong Xuan Market & night market💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

Dong Xuan Market is the biggest market hall of the Old Quarter and offers clothing, household goods, fabrics and food. At weekends there is a night market in the evening on the Old Quarter’s streets with food and souvenirs (check times beforehand).

Haggle in a friendly way and ask the price. Keep bags in front.

Tip: Come on weekend evenings.

Tay Ho (West Lake) & Tran Quoc Pagoda💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

West Lake in the north of the city is a calmer district with cafés, restaurants and a lakeside promenade. On a small peninsula in the lake stands the Tran Quoc Pagoda, one of Hanoi’s oldest pagodas. In the evening the sunset is beautiful.

You reach the lake by taxi or Grab in about 15 to 20 minutes from the Old Quarter.

Tip: Come at sunset.

Street food: bún chả, phở, bánh mì & more

Hanoi is a city for eating: phở bò in the morning, bún chả at midday, chả cá (fried fish with dill), bánh cuốn (rice rolls) and egg coffee. Sit on a plastic stool and order what locals eat.

Prices are usually low; ask the price anyway. Look for stalls with lots of business.

Tip: Choose stalls with lots of business.

Day trip: Ha Long Bay or Ninh Binh

From Hanoi you reach Ha Long Bay with its limestone cliffs (about three to four hours’ drive) or Ninh Binh with rice fields, rivers and karst mountains (about two hours). Both are possible by tour or train/bus.

Ninh Binh suits a day trip; for Ha Long an overnight stay is more worthwhile. Compare providers and prices beforehand.

Tip: Ninh Binh as a day trip, Ha Long with an overnight stay.

What makes Vietnam special

Mopeds everywhere, plastic stools by the roadside serving the country’s best food. Vietnam was under Chinese rule for about a thousand years, later French colonial rule – the baguettes for bánh mì are a legacy. The country was reunified in 1976.

FAQ

How do I travel between cities?

Best by plane – cheap.

How many days do you need in Hanoi?
Two to three days; with Ha Long or Ninh Binh rather four.
Is Hanoi safe?
Mostly yes; watch for pickpockets and traffic. Check current travel advice beforehand.
