Getting there and around

Arnhem lies on the Düsseldorf–Amsterdam rail line; check current connections beforehand. Buses run to Hoge Veluwe National Park; the white bikes are available on site (check current conditions and admission beforehand).

From NRW you reach Arnhem by train in a short time; Arnhem Centraal station lies directly on the line towards Utrecht and Amsterdam. Arnhem is the only city in the Netherlands with a trolleybus network, which connects the city centre with suburbs. You buy tickets via the OV chip card or by credit card when boarding.

For the Hoge Veluwe you need a bus or a car: buses run to the park entrances; at the entrance you buy the ticket and get a white bike. Plan the return time so you do not miss the last bus.

1. Must-sees

Hoge Veluwe National Park on the white bikes

Through the park on the white free bikes – heath, forest and dunes.

The Hoge Veluwe National Park, at around 5,400 hectares, is one of the largest nature parks in the Netherlands: heath, forest, sand dunes and open areas where red deer, mouflon and wild boar live. You pay admission at the entrance (for the park, not for the bike); afterwards you can use the white bicycles at the park entrances and bike stands free of charge and leave them at any other stand.

The cycle paths are mostly flat and well developed; for a loop to the museum and the hunting lodge allow about two to three hours. Bring water and something to eat, because there are only a few refreshment stops in the park. Check admission, opening times and conditions for the bikes beforehand.

Tip: Come early, take a bike and ride to the furthest target first – it gets busier in the afternoon.

The Bridge at Arnhem

One of the war’s most famous battles was fought around the Arnhem bridge in 1944.

The bridge over the Lower Rhine is today called the John Frost Bridge, named after the British lieutenant colonel who defended it in September 1944 during Operation Market Garden. The battle was part of the Allied attempt to capture the Rhine bridges and ended with an Allied defeat; it later became known through the film “A Bridge Too Far”.

Today you see the bridge from the bank; below there are memorial plaques and a monument. If you are interested in the history, combine the visit with the Airborne Museum in Oosterbeek. Around the anniversary in September there are commemorative events.

Kröller-Müller Museum & sculpture garden

In the middle of the national park lies the Kröller-Müller Museum with one of the largest Van Gogh collections in the world – after the Van Gogh Museum in Amsterdam it is considered the second largest. Works by Seurat, Mondrian, Picasso and others are added. The museum is quiet, bright and not overcrowded.

Behind the house extends the sculpture garden, one of the largest in Europe: over 160 works (according to the museum, roughly) in woodland and meadow, for example by Rodin and Henry Moore. With the white bike it is a few minutes away; plan two to three hours. Check admission and opening times beforehand; the museum is often closed on Mondays.

Tip: Go to the sculpture garden after the museum if you still have energy – it is especially lovely in sun.

Airborne Museum Hartenstein & Oosterbeek cemetery

In the suburb of Oosterbeek lies the Airborne Museum in the former Hotel Hartenstein, which was the British headquarters in September 1944. The exhibition tells the story of the battle with uniforms, weapons, documents and reconstructed scenes – a very good introduction if you want to understand the bridge.

Nearby lies the Airborne Cemetery, where fallen soldiers are buried. The place is quiet and moving. Get there by bus or bike from Arnhem; check opening times and admission beforehand.

Tip: Plan the visit before the bridge – then you see the place with different eyes.

Eusebius Church & old town

The large Eusebius Church dominates the old town with a tower about 93 metres high, reached by lift and stairs; at the top you have a view over Arnhem, the Rhine and the hills. Around the church lie the Korenmarkt, small lanes, cafés and shops.

The centre was heavily destroyed in the war and rebuilt afterwards. You see modern buildings next to restored remains such as the Sabelspoort, the last surviving city gate. Plan two to three hours for a walk.

Tip: The tower is not always open – check times beforehand and plan it for good weather.

Netherlands Open Air Museum

The Dutch Open Air Museum in Arnhem shows life in the Netherlands over centuries: farmhouses, workshops, mills, trams and village streets where performers demonstrate crafts. It lies in a large area with meadows and hills.

A visit is especially worthwhile with children or in bad weather, when you can retreat into houses. Plan three to four hours; please check admission and times. If you want to go to the Hoge Veluwe on the same day, it is better to choose one of the two.

Tip: Perfect for rainy days and families – plan three to four hours.

Burgers' Zoo

Burgers' Zoo is considered one of the best zoos in the Netherlands: instead of enclosures there are large landscape areas such as the rainforest “Bush”, the desert, the mangroves and the marine aquarium “Ocean”, in which you walk through the habitats. That makes the visit more impressive than in classic zoos.

Plan half a day to a full day; admission and opening times change, book online if needed. The zoo is especially good for families or in rain, because many areas are covered.

Tip: Come in the morning when the animals are more active and it is quieter.

📍 View in Google Maps

Sonsbeek Park

Sonsbeek Park is Arnhem’s large city park with ponds, waterfalls, old trees and meadows. Here you can walk, jog or sit in a café. In some years the park is also the focus of the Sonsbeek sculpture exhibition, which has taken place with contemporary art since 1949.

The park is a good place to let the day end quietly – especially in autumn when the trees change colour.

Tip: Pack a picnic and sit by one of the ponds.

FAQ

Are the white bikes really free?

Noel’s tip calls them free bikes; check the current conditions on site.

How many days do you need in Arnhem?
For city and Hoge Veluwe plan two days; if you only want the Veluwe bike tour, one day is enough.
Is the Kröller-Müller Museum worth it?
Yes, especially for Van Gogh fans and sculpture lovers; it is often quieter than the big museums in Amsterdam.
