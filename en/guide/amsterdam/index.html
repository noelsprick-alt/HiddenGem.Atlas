Amsterdam is a few hours from NRW and still feels like another world: canals, bicycles, gabled houses. The trick is to dodge the old-town crowds – get up early, switch neighbourhoods and take the free ferry across the IJ.

This guide shows must-sees, hidden gems and practical tips. Prices and times are guidelines (2026).

My story in Amsterdam

For me the Netherlands is the land of short distances – from NRW you are there in an hour, and every city feels different. In Amsterdam my advice is: the Jordaan early, while the canals are still yours, and lunch at the Foodhallen in an old tram depot. For the view from above: A'DAM Lookout – the brave take the swing over the edge.

Getting to Amsterdam and around

From NRW you take the train (ICE/IC via Arnhem) to Amsterdam Centraal in about two to three hours; check current connections beforehand. Schiphol airport has a direct rail link to the central station. In town, feet, tram and ferry are enough. The ferries behind the central station across the IJ are free.

Careful: cyclists move fast. Do not stop on the red asphalt to take photos.

1. Must-sees

Jordaan & canals in the morning

The Jordaan early, while the canals are still yours: small bridges, houseboats and cafés before the groups arrive. On Saturdays the Noordermarkt market is worth a visit.

📍 View in Google Maps

A'DAM Lookout

A tower in Amsterdam-Noord with a 360° view over the city, a bar and the “Over the Edge” swing above the roof edge. 4.6 stars on Google from over 31,000 reviews. Reach it with the free ferry from the central station.

🕒 daily about 10 am–10 pm according to Google

Tip: Visitors mention a surcharge for the swing (one guest: €30). Go up before sunset.

📍 View in Google Maps

Foodhallen

Lunch at the Foodhallen: a street-food hall in a restored tram depot. 4.4 stars on Google from over 23,000 reviews. Guests praise the variety (bitterballen, tacos, Indonesian dishes), some criticise prices and full tables.

🕒 daily about 12 pm–11 pm according to Google

Tip: Come early to get a seat.

2. Hidden gems

NDSM Wharf💎 Hidden gem

With the free ferry (line F4 behind the central station, about 15 minutes according to visitors) to the other side of the IJ: a former shipyard full of street art, bars and creative scene. Entry to the site is free. 4.5 stars on Google from nearly 10,000 reviews.

💶 free

📍 View in Google Maps

Oddities: cat boat & monkey bar

In Amsterdam there is a houseboat only for cats (the Poezenboot) and a bar where sailors supposedly paid with monkeys. I put both on my map – check opening times beforehand.

What makes the Netherlands special

There are more bicycles than inhabitants, and a large part of the country lies below sea level – protected by dikes, dunes and pumping stations. In the 17th century the small republic was a world power: the East India Company founded in 1602 is considered the world’s first joint-stock company, Rembrandt and Vermeer painted in this era. Amsterdam is the capital, but the government sits in The Hague.

Where to stay

  • Jordaan / canal ring: beautiful but expensive.
  • Amsterdam-Noord: quieter, close to NDSM and A'DAM Lookout, ferry free.
  • Near the central station: practical for arrival, touristy at night.

Amsterdam is expensive – book early.

FAQ

How long do you need in Amsterdam?

Two days for the highlights, three with museums and Noord.

Is the ferry to NDSM really free?

Yes, the IJ ferries behind the central station are free according to visitors.

Do I need a car?

No. Train, tram and ferry are enough, parking is expensive.
