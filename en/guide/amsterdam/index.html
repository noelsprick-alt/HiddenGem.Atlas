My story in Amsterdam

For me the Netherlands is the land of short distances – from NRW you are there in an hour, and every city feels different. In Amsterdam my advice is: the Jordaan early, while the canals are still yours, and lunch at the Foodhallen in an old tram depot. Getting to Amsterdam and around

From NRW you take the train (ICE/IC via Arnhem) to Amsterdam Centraal in about two to three hours; check current connections beforehand. Schiphol airport has a direct rail link to the central station. In town, feet, tram and ferry are enough. The ferries behind the central station across the IJ are free.

Careful: cyclists move fast. Do not stop on the red asphalt to take photos.

1. Must-sees

Jordaan & canals in the morning🆓 Free to experience

The Jordaan early, while the canals are still yours: small bridges, houseboats and cafés before the groups arrive. On Saturdays the Noordermarkt market is worth a visit.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

A'DAM Lookout

A tower in Amsterdam-Noord with a 360° view over the city, a bar and the “Over the Edge” swing above the roof edge. Reach it with the free ferry from the central station.

🕒 daily about 10 am–10 pm

Tip: The swing costs extra (around €30). Go up before sunset.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Foodhallen

Lunch at the Foodhallen: a street-food hall in a restored tram depot. The choice is huge – bitterballen, tacos, Indonesian dishes – prices are on the higher side and tables fill up fast at lunch.

🕒 daily about 12 pm–11 pm

Tip: Come early to get a seat.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Rijksmuseum

The Rijksmuseum shows Dutch art and history, including Rembrandt’s “Night Watch” and works by Vermeer. The building is worth seeing itself. The collection is large; choose highlights if you have little time.

Book tickets online; check times and prices beforehand.

Tip: Book tickets online and plan two to three hours.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Anne Frank House

The Anne Frank House is the annex where Anne Frank hid during the Second World War. The visit is moving. Tickets are available only online with a time slot and are often sold out weeks ahead (check current rules beforehand).

Plan the visit early and expect a quiet atmosphere.

Tip: Book weeks in advance.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Vondelpark🆓 Free to experience

Vondelpark is Amsterdam’s largest city park with ponds, lawns and cafés. In summer people picnic and there are concerts. A good break between museums.

Rent a bicycle and ride through the park.

Tip: Rent a bicycle.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Canal cruise

A canal cruise shows you Amsterdam from the water: bridges, gabled houses and houseboats. There are tours with audio guide, small boats and evening cruises. Evening cruises are atmospheric when the bridges are lit.

Compare providers and prices.

Tip: Choose an evening cruise.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Cycling in Amsterdam

Amsterdam is a cycling city; you can rent bikes and explore the city like locals. Watch for cycle paths, tram tracks and pedestrians. Cyclists often have priority, but traffic is dense.

Lock your bike well and follow the rules.

Tip: Always lock your bike well.

2. Hidden gems

NDSM Wharf💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

With the free ferry (line F4 behind the central station, about 15 minutes) to the other side of the IJ: a former shipyard full of street art, bars and creative scene. Entry to the site is free.

💶 free

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Oddities: cat boat & monkey bar

In Amsterdam there is a houseboat only for cats (the Poezenboot) and a bar where sailors supposedly paid with monkeys. I put both on my map – check opening times beforehand.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Begijnhof – quiet courtyard💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

The Begijnhof is a historic courtyard with small houses, a garden and the oldest surviving wooden house façade in the city, right in the centre. It is quiet and a place to catch your breath. Residents live here; be quiet.

You enter the courtyard through a small passage.

Tip: Be quiet – people live here.

De Pijp & Albert Cuyp Market💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

De Pijp is a lively district with cafés, restaurants and the Albert Cuyp Market, where stroopwafels, cheese, fish and flowers are sold (check market days beforehand). It feels less touristy.

Try fresh stroopwafels.

Tip: Try fresh stroopwafels.

Day trip: Zaanse Schans, Haarlem or Zandvoort

From Amsterdam you reach Zaanse Schans with windmills, Haarlem with its old town and Zandvoort with a beach in about 20 to 30 minutes (check timetables beforehand). All three are easy to reach by train or bus.

Plan half a day to a whole day.

Tip: Plan half a day.

Dutch cuisine: stroopwafel, bitterballen, herring

Try stroopwafels, bitterballen (fried ragout balls), fries with mayo, poffertjes and – for the brave – raw herring with onions. Many brown cafés serve beer and snacks. Cheese like Gouda and Edam is available everywhere.

Prices change; ask beforehand.

Tip: Try bitterballen in a brown café.

What makes the Netherlands special

There are more bicycles than inhabitants, and a large part of the country lies below sea level – protected by dikes, dunes and pumping stations. In the 17th century the small republic was a world power: the East India Company founded in 1602 is considered the world’s first joint-stock company, Rembrandt and Vermeer painted in this era. Amsterdam is the capital, but the government sits in The Hague.

FAQ

How long do you need in Amsterdam?

Two days for the highlights, three with museums and Noord.

Is the ferry to NDSM really free?

Yes, the IJ ferries behind the central station are free.

Do I need a car?

No. Train, tram and ferry are enough, parking is expensive.
