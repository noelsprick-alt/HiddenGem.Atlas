Getting there and around

You reach Freiburg by ICE or regional train; the old town is small and car-free.

Freiburg lies at the foot of the Black Forest and is easy to reach by train from Karlsruhe, Basel or Offenburg (check timetables beforehand). The old town is small and car-free; you walk or use the tram. Bikes are everyday transport in Freiburg – rental bikes are available at the station.

The station is about a 10-minute walk from the old town. Parking in the city centre is expensive; use the car parks on the edge.

1. Must-sees

Münstermarkt🆓 Free to experience

On Saturday a “Lange Rote” sausage in your hand.

The Münstermarkt on the cathedral square is the heartbeat of the city: farmers from the region sell fruit, vegetables, cheese, flowers and sausage; the “Lange Rote”, a bratwurst in a bread roll, is the classic. The market takes place Monday to Saturday, and on Saturday it is biggest and liveliest (check times beforehand).

Behind the stalls the cathedral rises – that makes the picture especially beautiful. Come in the morning before the midday crowd, and bring cash.

Tip: Come early on Saturday, eat a Lange Rote and go up the Schlossberg afterwards.

Schlossberg🆓 Free to experience

With a view over the Minster and the Black Forest. My tip: walk up or take the Schlossbergbahn; the tower is 35 metres high.

The Schlossberg is Freiburg’s local mountain on the eastern edge of the old town. You can walk up (about 20–30 minutes) or take the Schlossberg railway. At the top there are viewpoints, a café and the tower. From there you see over the old town, the cathedral and the Black Forest.

Sunset is especially beautiful when the roofs glow red. Bring a jacket; a fresh wind blows at the top. The tower is just under 35 metres high and offers additional perspectives.

Tip: Go up at sunset and take water.

Freiburg Minster & tower

Freiburg Minster is a Gothic masterpiece with a tower about 116 metres high whose openwork spire is considered unique. The art historian Jacob Burckhardt once called it “the most beautiful tower in Christendom”. Inside you find stained glass, sculptures and altars from several centuries.

You can climb the tower (about 330 steps; check times and admission beforehand) and see the roofs and the Black Forest at the top. Plan about an hour for church and tower.

Tip: Climb the tower in the morning, then the view is often clearer.

Historical Merchants’ Hall, town hall & city gates

The Historical Merchants’ Hall on the cathedral square is a red building from the 16th century with arcades and coats of arms. Town hall, Martinstor and Schwabentor also belong to the beautiful buildings of the old town. Around them you find cafés, shops and squares.

A walk with these stops takes about two hours and leads you through the most important lanes. The town hall square is a lovely place for a break, especially in summer.

Tip: Start at the cathedral square and walk a loop through the old town.

Augustiner Museum

The Augustinermuseum in a former monastery shows medieval art, paintings and sculptures from south-west Germany. Especially worth seeing are the original minster figures and the stained glass. The museum is good for a rainy day.

Plan one to two hours; admission and opening times change (check beforehand, often closed on Mondays).

Tip: A perfect rainy-day tip close to the minster.

2. Hidden gems

Die Bächle💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

The small water channels in the lanes have existed since the Middle Ages. Whoever steps in will supposedly marry someone from Freiburg.

The Freiburg Bächle are narrow water channels that run through the old town. In the Middle Ages they served fire-fighting and water supply for animals. The superstition says: whoever steps in by accident marries a Freiburger. Children splash in them, dogs drink.

Watch the ground while walking – especially on squares. Photograph the Bächle best in sun when the water glitters.

Tip: Watch the ground – and laugh if you step in by accident.

Gerberau & Fischerau – the canal quarter💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

In Gerberau and Fischerau, on the east side of the old town, Bächle and small canals run between half-timbered houses and cafés. Tanners and fishermen once worked here; today you find bars, restaurants and small shops. It is quieter than the main streets and very photogenic.

In the evening the district feels especially atmospheric. Sit in a place by the water and let the day end.

Tip: Come in the evening for atmosphere and lights.

Vauban – eco district💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

The Vauban district in the south of the city is considered a showcase project for sustainable living: passive houses, solar estates, low-car streets, lots of green. It is a quarter that arose on former barracks land and is today a lively district with cafés and small shops.

You reach it by tram; plan half an afternoon. It is a good destination if urban planning and sustainability interest you.

Tip: Take the tram there and eat in one of the cafés.

Schauinsland – cable car & views

The Schauinsland is Freiburg’s local mountain in the south at about 1,280 metres. The Schauinsland cable car runs up from Freiburg-Günterstal (check times and prices beforehand). At the top there is a lookout tower, hiking trails and a wide view over the Rhine plain, the Vosges and the Black Forest.

In summer it is much cooler at the top than in the city; in winter there is often snow. Plan half a day.

Tip: Go up in clear weather and bring a jacket.

Kaiserstuhl – vineyards on the doorstep💎 Hidden gem

About 30 minutes west of Freiburg lies the Kaiserstuhl, a volcanic massif with vineyards, terraces and small villages. The region is among the warmest in Germany and known for Spätburgunder and Grauburgunder. You can hike on marked paths and stop at wineries or Straußwirtschaften.

By car, bike or train the trip is easy; check timetables beforehand. A lovely contrast to the city.

Tip: Plan an afternoon and taste wine on site.

FAQ

When is the Münstermarkt?

On Saturdays – check times beforehand.

How many days do you need in Freiburg?
One day is enough for the old town, two days with the Schauinsland and Kaiserstuhl.
What does “Bächle” mean?
Bächle are the narrow water channels in the lanes of the old town; since the Middle Ages they have been a landmark of the city.
