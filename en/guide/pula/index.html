Pula sits at the tip of Istria, and a Roman amphitheatre stands between a traffic light and a bus stop. Add rocky coast instead of sandy beach, a market hall with the day’s catch and a hinterland of truffles, olive oil and Malvazija wine.

This guide takes you through antiquity, coast and surroundings, with getting there, guideline prices (2026) and a plan for 3 to 7 days. Check opening hours and admissions beforehand.

My story in Pula

A week in Pula in October 2025 – and in the end I had more photos of sunsets than of the arena. I stood between the arches, the last light fell through the upper windows and ordinary life went on below. In the evening it is lit up and looks as if someone had moved the Colosseum to the Adriatic. Right by the arena a DJ played on a terrace while the sun disappeared behind the rooftops. Antiquity behind me, beats in my ears. The rest of the week I spent on the coast: rock slabs instead of sand, waves crashing against the stones. I climbed into a hole in the rocks simply because it was there, and on the last evening I stood on the stones until only a silhouette of me was left.

Getting to Pula and around

Arrival

Pula has its own airport (PUY) with seasonal connections from Germany. It is about 6 km into town – taxi or bus; check current lines and prices beforehand. Long-distance buses reach Pula from Zagreb, Rijeka, Trieste and Ljubljana. By car Istria is easy to reach by motorway, tolls apply.

In town

The old town is small and walkable. For Verudela and Kamenjak a scooter, bike or car is worthwhile; buses run to Verudela, more often in summer.

1. Antiquity in the middle of town

Pula Arena

One of the best-preserved Roman amphitheatres in the world, built in the 1st century and right in the middle of the city. On Google it has 4.6 stars from over 76,000 reviews. Inside you can move freely; the basement has an exhibition (including one on olive oil).

🕒 daily about 8 am–9 pm according to Google (seasonal) · 💶 about €10 according to visitors

Tip: In the evening the arena is lit up – come at sunset and see the light afterwards. In summer there are concerts.

📍 View in Google Maps

Forum & Temple of Augustus

The old Roman forum is now a square with cafés. I sat there with a coffee right opposite the Temple of Augustus – it has stood for about 2,000 years. Inside there is a small collection of Roman sculptures for about €2. 4.6 stars on Google.

📍 View in Google Maps

Zerostrasse tunnels & citadel💎 Hidden gem

A two-level tunnel system beneath the citadel with photos and cool air – perfect on hot days. According to visitors the ticket includes the fortress above. 4.4 stars on Google.

🕒 daily about 9 am–5 pm according to Google

Tip: The info boards cover a lot of city history including trams – take it as a cool break rather than a museum.

📍 View in Google Maps

Market hall (Tržnica)

A few streets on, the market hall has the day’s catch on ice, and oranges are stacked outside. The iron hall opened in 1903; inside there is fish and meat, bistros upstairs, outside fruit, truffles, olive oil and honey. 4.5 stars on Google.

🕒 daily about 7 am–1 pm according to Google

📍 View in Google Maps

The quiet cloister courtyard💎 Hidden gem

In the middle of the old town a cloister courtyard full of roses is hidden, where you forget that a city lies outside.

2. Coast: rocks instead of sand

Verudela💎 Hidden gem

Rock slabs instead of sandy beach, waves crashing against the stones and evenings when the sky turns first orange and then violet. 4.7 stars on Google; visitors praise the clear water but note the stones.

Tip: Bring water shoes. Come for sunset – and wear sturdy shoes for the rocks.

📍 View in Google Maps

Fort Bourguignon

One of the Austrian fortresses built around Pula in the second half of the 19th century (originally Fort Monsival, built 1861–66) – lost-place flair on the edge of Verudela. 4.1 stars on Google.

Tip: A place for the curious; watch your footing and do not enter closed-off areas.

📍 View in Google Maps

Cape Kamenjak

A peninsula at Istria’s southern tip with many small coves, turquoise water and hiking trails. 4.5 stars on Google. Visitors report an admission of roughly €10–20 for cars, on foot or by bike it is free; the gravel tracks are dusty.

Tip: Pack water shoes and a parasol – there is little shade. Come early and explore by bike.

3. Istria: Hum, truffles & wine

Hum – the smallest town in the world💎 Hidden gem

In the hinterland stands Hum, which calls itself the smallest town in the world, with about 30 residents. Guests mention the Glagolitic script, a small museum, liqueur and jam to taste and the view. 4.7 stars on Google.

Tip: Combine Hum with Motovun (truffles) as a day trip by car or tour – it is hard by public transport.

📍 View in Google Maps

Food & drink

  • Fish and seafood: fresh from the market hall or in a harbour restaurant.
  • Truffles from the woods around Motovun – in pasta or with egg.
  • Olive oil and Malvazija, the Istrian white wine.
  • Sundowner at the arena: terrace, DJ, sunset.

Fish dishes cost clearly more than pizza and pasta – ask for the price per kilo.

Where to stay

  • Old town: everything on foot, close to arena, forum and market.
  • Verudela: coast, hotels and apartments, quieter – rocks and sunsets in the evening.
  • Premantura: close to Cape Kamenjak, for nature fans with a car or bike.

What makes Istria special

The Romans made Pula a colony, the arena was built in the 1st century. Later Venice ruled, and in the 19th century Pula became the main war port of the Austro-Hungarian navy. In 1904/05 James Joyce taught English here. After the Second World War the city passed from Italy to Yugoslavia, and since 1991 it has belonged to Croatia.

My conclusion: Pula is not a place to tick off – half a day for antiquity is enough, the rest belongs to the coast.

FAQ

How many days do you need in Pula?

1–2 days cover the city, 3–5 with coast and surroundings. Noel stayed a week.

Are there sandy beaches in Pula?

Hardly – the coast is mostly rock and pebbles. Water shoes make sense.

Is a rental car worthwhile?

For Kamenjak, Hum and Motovun yes. You can manage the city and Verudela without a car.

When is the best time to go?

May–June and September–October: warm enough, less crowded. Noel was there in October 2025.
