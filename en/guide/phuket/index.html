Phuket is Thailand’s largest island and for many the entry to the country. There are plenty of beaches – but the best moments are often away from them: at the market, in a shrine, in a canoe.

Prices and times are guidelines (2026).

My story in Phuket

Almost three weeks in Thailand in February 2026: first Phuket, then Krabi. I had expected beach. The Big Buddha sits 45 metres high in white marble above the island – from below he looks almost unreal. In Phuket’s old town I stumbled between colourful Sino-Portuguese houses into a Chinese shrine with golden dragons and incense sticks. And at the market there they were: fried crickets and silkworm pupae, paid by the spoonful. Next to them durian, dragon fruit and longan, as if it were all the most normal thing in the world. Getting to Phuket and around

Phuket has an international airport served from Bangkok and other hubs. On the island scooters, taxi apps and songthaews are common; agree taxi prices beforehand. If you ride a scooter you need a valid licence and a helmet – the risk is real, inform yourself beforehand.

Phuket airport lies in the north of the island; taxis, Grab and minibuses take you to the beaches (check prices beforehand; travel time 30 to 90 minutes depending on destination). On the island you need a taxi, Grab, scooter or rental car; note left-hand traffic. The currency is the Thai baht.

The rainy season is roughly between May and October.

1. Must-sees

Big Buddha🆓 Free to experience

45 metres high in white marble above the island. Admission is free; dress appropriately (shoulders and knees covered).

The Big Buddha sits on Nakkerd Hill in the south of the island: 45 metres high, made of white marble, with a wide view over Chalong, Kata and Karon. The way up leads by road; at the top there are small shops, bells and temple grounds. Entry is free; donations are welcome.

Wear clothing that covers shoulders and knees. Come in the morning or late afternoon when it is cooler.

🕒 daily about 6 am–6:30 pm

Tip: Come in the morning or late afternoon and cover shoulders and knees.

Wat Chalong🆓 Free to experience

Wat Chalong is the most important temple on the island with golden Buddhas, a pagoda and colourful halls. Visitors apply gold leaf and light incense. The temple lies near the Big Buddha and combines well with it.

Take off your shoes and dress respectfully.

Tip: Combine the visit with the Big Buddha.

Phuket Old Town – Thalang Road & Soi Romanee

The old town has Sino-Portuguese houses in pastel colours, cafés, galleries and street art. Soi Romanee is the most colourful lane. On Sundays there is a market on Thalang Road with food and crafts (check times beforehand).

In the evening stroll here rather than on Bangla Road – calmer, prettier, and the food is cheap.

Tip: Come on Sundays for the market.

Promthep Cape & sunset🆓 Free to experience

Promthep Cape at the very south is considered the island’s most popular sunset spot. From the rocks you look at the sea, and in the evening the viewpoint fills. Small stalls sell snacks and drinks.

Come early to find a spot. Check the sunset time beforehand.

Tip: Come an hour before sunset.

Karon, Kata & southern beaches

Karon, Kata and Kata Noi beaches lie in the south-west and are less crowded than Patong. There are sun loungers, restaurants and surf waves in the rainy season. In the rainy season currents are dangerous – watch the flags.

The best travel time for beaches is November to April.

Tip: Watch the flags and the current.

2. Hidden gems & experiences

Phang Nga Bay by canoe💎 Hidden gem

Limestone cliffs, caves and calm water: a canoe tour through the bay is the highlight. Tours start early in the morning and last until late afternoon.

Tip: Book early; the good slots go quickly.

Boat tour: Phi Phi Islands & island hopping💎 Hidden gem

From Phuket boats go to the Phi Phi Islands, Koh Racha and other islands. Tours start early in the morning and last until the late afternoon. Snorkelling is included on many tours. Maya Bay has visiting rules that change (check beforehand).

Compare providers, pay attention to safety and small groups. Prices change.

Tip: Book early and look for small groups.

Elephant sanctuary & ethical animal care💎 Hidden gem

If you want to see elephants, choose a sanctuary that does not allow riding and focuses on animal welfare. There you observe elephants in a natural environment. Find out about the provider’s standards beforehand; bookings are usually needed in advance.

Avoid shows and riding.

Tip: Choose places without riding and shows.

Thai cuisine: pad thai, mango sticky rice & night markets

Try pad thai, tom yum, massaman curry, mango sticky rice and fresh fruit. Night markets like the Naka Market or the Sunday market in the old town offer cheap food. Say how spicy you want it.

Prices are usually low; ask beforehand.

Tip: Say how spicy you want it.

What makes Thailand special

Thailand is the only country in Southeast Asia never colonised by a European power – people are proud of that to this day. Over 90 percent are Buddhists, and small spirit houses with offerings stand outside houses and shops everywhere. Until 1939 the country was called Siam.

My verdict: Phuket for the start, Krabi for the landscape. See my Krabi guide.
