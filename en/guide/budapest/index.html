Getting there and around

You reach Budapest by train (e.g. from Vienna or Bratislava), flight or coach; check current connections beforehand.

Budapest airport lies about 30 minutes from the centre; bus 100E, taxi and train connect it with the city (check prices beforehand). In the city you use metro, tram, bus and ferries; the city centre is easy to explore on foot. The currency is the forint.

Tip: use official taxis or apps to avoid inflated prices.

1. Must-sees

Széchenyi Baths

Into the baths in the morning. My tip: a weekday morning (calmer); bring flip-flops and a towel.

The Széchenyi Bath is one of Europe’s largest thermal baths with a yellow neo-Baroque façade, outdoor pools with warm water and saunas. It is especially beautiful in the morning or in winter when steam rises over the pools. There are changing rooms, cabins and lockers.

Bring swimwear, a towel and bath shoes. Book tickets online; check prices and times beforehand.

🕒 about 7 am–8 pm (Fri until 10 pm)

Tip: Come on a weekday morning and book online.

Fisherman’s Bastion & Matthias Church🆓 Free to experience

On Castle Hill stand the neo-Romanesque Fisherman’s Bastion with its towers and the Matthias Church with a colourful roof. From the terrace you look over the Danube and at the Parliament. Parts of the bastion are freely accessible, others cost admission (check beforehand).

Come early in the morning or at sunset.

Tip: Come early or at sunset.

Buda Castle & Chain Bridge

Buda Castle is a large complex with museums and gardens. The Chain Bridge connects Buda and Pest and is lit in the evening. You can go up by funicular or on foot.

Walk across the bridge in the evening.

Tip: Walk across the bridge in the evening.

Great Market Hall🆓 Free to experience

The Great Market Hall is a 19th-century building with fruit, paprika, sausage, spices and souvenirs. Upstairs there is Hungarian street food like lángos, goulash and kürtőskalács. Prices are partly touristy.

Try lángos and compare prices.

Tip: Try lángos and compare prices.

Danube promenade & Shoes on the Danube Bank🆓 Free to experience

Along the Danube on the Pest side runs a promenade with a view of the castle and bridges. The “Shoes on the Danube Bank” memorial commemorates the victims of the Holocaust. A quiet place to pause.

Be respectful.

2. Crazy places

Hospital in the Rock💎 Hidden gem

A secret hospital in the rock beneath the castle that was a state secret until 2002. Guided tour only (about 50–60 minutes); no photography.

Gellért Hill & Citadella💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

Gellért Hill offers one of the best views over the city. At the top stand the Citadella and the Liberty Statue. You can walk up (about 20 to 30 minutes) and be at the top at sunset.

Take water.

Tip: Go up at sunset.

Jewish Quarter & Great Synagogue💎 Hidden gem🆓 Free to experience

In the Jewish Quarter lies the Great Synagogue, the largest in Europe, next to memorials, cafés and ruin bars. It is a district with history and nightlife. Check times and prices of the synagogue beforehand.

Go to the synagogue by day and to the bars in the evening.

Tip: Go to the synagogue by day.

Gellért & Rudas thermal baths💎 Hidden gem

Besides the Széchenyi Bath there is the Gellért Bath in Art Nouveau style and the Rudas Bath with Turkish domes and a roof terrace overlooking the Danube. Check prices and rules (for example separate days) beforehand.

A thermal bath is a good break after a sightseeing day.

Tip: Check rules and prices beforehand.

📍 Open & save in Google Maps

Hungarian cuisine: goulash, lángos & kürtőskalács

Try goulash soup, chicken paprikash, lángos (fried flatbread with garlic and cheese), kürtőskalács (chimney cake) and Dobos cake. Wines like Tokaji or Egri Bikavér go with it.

Prices change; ask beforehand.

Tip: Try goulash and lángos.

What makes Budapest special

Over a hundred thermal springs bubble beneath the city. Budapest has existed as one city only since 1873, when Buda, Pest and Óbuda were united. The parliament is Hungary’s largest building.
