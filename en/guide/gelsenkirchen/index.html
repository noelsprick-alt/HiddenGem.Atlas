Zollverein Colliery in Essen

Zollverein Colliery in Essen is a UNESCO World Heritage site and regarded as one of Europe’s most beautiful industrial monuments: winding tower, coking plant, halls and the Ruhr Museum in the former coal washery, reached by a long escalator. The site is spacious; you can take tours or simply stroll.

The site is easy to reach from Gelsenkirchen by train. Plan half a day; check times and prices of the museums beforehand.

Tip: Plan half a day and look at the escalator to the Ruhr Museum.

Nordsternpark & Hercules🆓 Free to experience

Nordsternpark in Gelsenkirchen was created on the site of a former colliery. Here there are paths, green areas, the winding tower and a large statue of Hercules by the artist Markus Lüpertz, visible far across the area. The park shows how industrial land becomes recreation space.

A good place for a walk or a bike ride along the Rhine-Herne Canal (check access and times beforehand).

Tip: Combine the park with a bike ride along the canal.

Schalke tour & club museum

Besides match day you can visit the Veltins-Arena on a stadium tour and experience the history of FC Schalke 04 in the club museum. The “Königsblauen” are a fixed part of the city’s identity. Check tour and museum times beforehand.

Even if you are not a football fan the visit gives an insight into how much the club means to people here.

Tip: Check tour and museum times beforehand.

What makes the Ruhr special

For over 150 years the Ruhr was Germany’s industrial heart. In December 2018 the last hard-coal mine closed in Bottrop. Zeche Zollverein in Essen has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2001, in 2010 the Ruhr was European Capital of Culture. Mines became museums, spoil tips viewpoints, blast furnaces climbing parks.
