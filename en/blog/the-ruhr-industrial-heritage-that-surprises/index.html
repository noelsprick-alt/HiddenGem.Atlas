The Ruhr: industrial heritage that surprises

Gelsenkirchen is my home town. If you want to understand what makes the Ruhr tick, you do not need a guidebook - just a match day, a slag heap at sunset and a currywurst at the kiosk.

What makes the Ruhr special

Honest, direct, warm-hearted - and proud of a history of coal and steel. Collieries became museums, slag heaps became viewpoints with artworks, blast furnaces became climbing parks. And on every corner there is a kiosk ("Bude") for your beer and bag of crisps.

A little history

For more than 150 years the Ruhr was Germany's industrial heart, with hundreds of thousands of miners working underground. In December 2018 Prosper-Haniel in Bottrop, Germany's last hard-coal mine, closed. The Zollverein colliery in Essen has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2001, and in 2010 the whole Ruhr was European Capital of Culture.

My experience

On a match day go to the arena in Gelsenkirchen - royal blue and goosebumps, even if you are not a football fan. Then head up the Rheinelbe slag heap at sunset to the Himmelstreppe, a stairway of concrete blocks that simply ends in the sky.

My tips in the Ruhr

Duisburg-Nord Landscape Park at night, when the old ironworks is lit up in colour. Tiger & Turtle, a roller coaster you walk. The roof of the Gasometer in Oberhausen. And the Centre for International Light Art in Unna, deep in the cellars of an old brewery. You will find more than 60 places in the Ruhr on my map.
