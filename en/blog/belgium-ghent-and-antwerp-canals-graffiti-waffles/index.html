Belgium: Ghent & Antwerp – canals, graffiti, waffles

Everyone knows Bruges. Ghent makes far fewer lists – wrongly, as I found out in January 2026. Two days of canals, graffiti and waffles – and a glance over to Antwerp.

The Graslei at night

On the very first evening: guild houses reflected in black water, lights on the façades, the quays almost empty. The next day tour boats glide through the canals and the Graslei is buzzing.

An alley anyone can spray

Werregarenstraatje is Ghent's legal graffiti alley. The walls get repainted constantly – while I was there, an artist with a paint bucket and a red stool was simply carrying on at the wall. Whatever I photographed has probably long been painted over.

Waffle, matcha, Aperol

Of course there was a Belgian waffle, with a little flag on top. Then matcha in a small café near the cathedral, a colourful design shop full of ceramics and an Aperol in the winter sun on the Korenmarkt. In front of the city hall with its flags I spent ages hunting for the perfect angle.

And Antwerp?

Antwerp is bigger and livelier: the Grote Markt with the Brabo fountain, grand guild houses, lots of fashion, lots of pace. By train the two cities are only about an hour apart – perfect for a long weekend.

My verdict

Ghent is ideal for a weekend: compact, walkable, with enough street art and cafés for two full days. Bruges is still on my list.
