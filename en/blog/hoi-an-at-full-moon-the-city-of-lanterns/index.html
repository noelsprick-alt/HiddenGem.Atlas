Hoi An at full moon: the city of lanterns

Once a month, at full moon, the lights go out in Hoi An's old town - and thousands of lanterns come on. I was there in February 2026, and this alone is worth the trip.

What makes Hoi An special

The old town is car-free, yellow merchants' houses line the river, and nowhere in Vietnam can you have a suit tailored so fast. Then there are dishes you only find here - cao lầu, for example, noodles that tradition says only turn out right with water from one particular old well.

A little history

From the 15th to the 19th century Hoi An was one of Southeast Asia's most important trading ports: Japanese, Chinese and European merchants lived side by side - the Japanese Bridge still recalls it. Then the river silted up, trade moved on and the town remained as if frozen in time. That is exactly what saved it: the old town has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1999.

My experience

Between Hanoi and Saigon, Hoi An was my middle stop. At full moon thousands of lanterns glow in the car-free old town - for me the reason the whole trip was worth it.

My tip

Plan your visit around the 14th day of the lunar month - the date is in my events calendar. In the evening set a candle afloat on the river in a paper lantern. And grab a bánh mì at Bánh mì Phượng, which Anthony Bourdain once called the best of his life.
