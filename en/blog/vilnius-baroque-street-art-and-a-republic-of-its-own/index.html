Vilnius: baroque, street art and a republic of its own

Vilnius is the surprise of the Baltics: a baroque old town that can easily keep up with Prague - just without the crowds. And in the middle of it a neighbourhood that declared itself a republic of its own.

What makes Lithuania special

Lithuania is the country where basketball is almost a religion, where people eat kepta duona (fried garlic bread) with their beer and where hot-air balloons may fly over the capital's old town on summer evenings. Vilnius old town is one of the largest surviving in Eastern Europe and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

A little history

Legend has it that Grand Duke Gediminas dreamt of an iron wolf and then founded the city. In the 15th century the Grand Duchy of Lithuania was one of the largest states in Europe. For centuries Vilnius was a centre of Jewish life, the "Jerusalem of the North". In 1989 two million people formed a human chain from Vilnius to Tallinn, and in 1990 Lithuania was the first Soviet republic to declare independence.

My tip

A walk through Užupis - the self-proclaimed artists' republic even has its own constitution on the wall ("A cat is not obliged to love its owner"). Then up Gediminas Tower for sunset.

Crazy places in and around Vilnius

The world's first Frank Zappa monument (Zappa never went to Lithuania), the writers' lane with hundreds of small artworks on its walls, the KGB museum with its real cells - and further afield the Hill of Crosses and Grūtas Park, where a mushroom farmer collected the toppled Soviet statues. All on my map.
