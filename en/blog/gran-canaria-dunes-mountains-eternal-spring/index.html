Gran Canaria: dunes, mountains, eternal spring

Gran Canaria is often called a "miniature continent" - and it is true: desert in the morning, pine forest at midday, harbour in the evening. I have been to the island, and it is exactly this variety that stays with you.

What makes Gran Canaria special

Around 20 to 25 degrees all year round, the dunes of Maspalomas in the south and Roque Nublo in the centre, a rock finger at around 1,800 metres. Because the island is so steep, climate and landscape change after just a few kilometres.

A little history

Long before the Spanish, the ancient Canarians lived here - their painted cave in Gáldar was discovered by farmers in the 19th century. At the end of the 15th century Castile conquered the island. In 1492 Columbus stopped in Las Palmas on his first voyage to America; the house where he is said to have stayed is now a museum.

My tip

Sunrise in the dunes of Maspalomas, the mountains and Roque Nublo in the afternoon and fish in the harbour of Puerto de Mogán in the evening. If you want more: the Cueva Pintada in Gáldar, a place most beach holidaymakers never see.
