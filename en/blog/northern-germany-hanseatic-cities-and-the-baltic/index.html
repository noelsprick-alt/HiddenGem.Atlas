Northern Germany: Hanseatic cities and the Baltic

Hamburg, Bremen, Lübeck, Rügen: for me the north means rough air, red brick and fish sandwiches. I have been to all four - and every corner tells of the time when Europe's most powerful merchants sat here.

What makes the north special

A "Moin" works at any time of day. The houses are red brick, the weather changes by the hour, and the water is never far away: the Elbe, the Weser, the Trave, the Baltic.

A little history

In the Middle Ages the Hanseatic League dominated trade in the North Sea and Baltic - Lübeck was its "queen". The Holstentor of 1478 is still the city's landmark. In Bremen the stone Roland has stood guard over the city's freedom in the market square since 1404. And Hamburg's Speicherstadt, the largest historic warehouse district in the world, has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2015.

My tips

Hamburg: the fish market very early on Sunday, then the ferry from the Landungsbrücken - a harbour tour at public-transport prices. Bremen: the Schnoor quarter and Böttcherstraße, and the Schlachte at sunset. Lübeck: the hidden alleys and courtyards of the old town and marzipan at Niederegger. Rügen: walk along the chalk cliffs to the Königsstuhl.

Crazy but true

In Bremen there is a manhole cover that answers with animal noises when you drop in a coin. The Ratskeller holds a wine from 1653. And on Rügen stands a 4.5-kilometre concrete building that was never finished: Prora. All on my map.
