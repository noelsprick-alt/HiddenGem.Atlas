Vietnam north to south - my route

Hanoi, Hoi An, Saigon: in February 2026 I travelled through Vietnam from north to south. Three cities, three completely different faces of one country - and more than 70 posts that came out of it.

What makes Vietnam special

Scooters, everywhere. Plastic stools at the roadside on which the best food in the country is served. Coffee with sweetened condensed milk or even egg - Vietnam is one of the largest coffee producers in the world. And people who smile at you even though you are blocking their pavement.

A little history

Vietnam was under Chinese rule for around a thousand years and later under French colonial rule - the baguettes in bánh mì are a legacy of that. After decades of war the country was reunified in 1976. With the reforms from 1986 came the boom you can feel on every street in Saigon today.

My experience: Hanoi

This is how I got to know Hanoi: pho at 6 am on a plastic chair - better than any restaurant. Early in the morning people do tai chi at Hoan Kiem Lake, and in the evening you drink a bia hơi in the old quarter, freshly brewed and for small change.

Hoi An and Saigon

In Hoi An thousands of lanterns glow in the car-free old town at full moon - that alone makes the trip worth it. Saigon was the opposite: the city never stops, and that is exactly the appeal. My advice there: make no plans, drift through District 1 and stroll along Nguyễn Huệ in the evening.

My tip

Flying between the cities is best - domestic flights are cheap. And try the classics at the source: egg coffee at Café Giảng in Hanoi, where it was invented in 1946, and bún chả at the place where Obama and Bourdain sat on plastic stools in 2016.
