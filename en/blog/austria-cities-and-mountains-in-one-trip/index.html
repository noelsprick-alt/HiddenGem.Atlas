Austria: cities and mountains in one trip

Austria isn't just any holiday country for me: I worked at a hotel reception in Bad Gastein. A town with a waterfall roaring through its centre, surrounded by Belle Époque hotels that look as if they're from another era.

Lungau: the mountain lake at 1,968 metres

In August 2026 I headed to the Lungau, one of the quietest corners of the Salzburg region. The trail leads over meadows and ridges, there's a summit cross at the top, and below it lies Landawirsee – crystal clear and, let's say, refreshing. I went in anyway. All the way. Head first.

Hut, Kaiserschmarrn, alpine pasture

Afterwards: Kaiserschmarrn at the Landawirseehütte with a cold local beer. The night was spent at the hut. And on the way back a herd of white cows with their calves stood in the middle of the pasture, watching us very closely.

And the cities?

Vienna, Salzburg, Innsbruck – each with its own character, all worth the trip. But if you only have a weekend: go to the mountains. A lake at almost 2,000 metres beats any shopping street.

What makes Austria special

Around two thirds of the country is Alps. Viennese coffee-house culture is part of UNESCO's intangible cultural heritage, and hardly anywhere else will you get a Kaiserschmarrn at a hut at 2,000 metres that tastes like it does here.

A little history

For more than 600 years the Habsburgs ruled from Vienna over an empire that at times stretched from Spain to Ukraine - until 1918. In the 19th century Bad Gastein was the spa of emperors and kings, and Empress Sisi came for cures. Austria has been neutral since 1955.

My tip

Landawirsee requires sure footing and good boots. Plan the hut as a stop – and bring a towel. You'll want to go in.
