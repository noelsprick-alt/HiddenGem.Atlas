Budapest: baths, ruin bars and Danube views

Budapest is best experienced in swimming trunks and, in the evening, with a beer in a half-derelict bar. I have been there - and hardly any city does contrasts as well in a single day.

What makes Budapest special

More than a hundred thermal springs bubble beneath the city - hence the many baths, from Ottoman to neo-baroque. And in empty old buildings the ruin bars appeared from the 2000s: junk furniture, fairy lights, courtyards full of people.

A little history

Budapest has only existed as one city since 1873 - when Buda, Pest and Óbuda were united. The Parliament on the Danube is the largest building in Hungary. In 1956 Hungarians rose up against the Soviet Union; the uprising was crushed, but the crowd toppled Stalin's statue - only his boots were left standing.

My tip

Széchenyi Baths in the morning, a ruin bar like Szimpla Kert in the evening - you will hardly find a bigger contrast in one day. And go down to the Danube once after dark, when the Parliament is lit up.

Crazy places

A secret hospital in the rock beneath the castle that was a state secret until 2002. A forest railway run by children. And a pinball museum where one ticket lets you play every machine. All on my map.
