Albania: Europe's last secret

For decades Albania was one of the most isolated countries in Europe. Today it's one of the most exciting. In May 2026 I was in Tirana and Durrës – two cities less than an hour apart, and still two different worlds.

Tirana: bunkers and matcha

Just a few steps from Skanderbeg Square, Bunk'Art 2 remembers the era of the dictatorship – a former bunker, now a museum and memorial to the victims of the secret police. A few streets away, in Blloku, once the sealed-off quarter of the party elite, people now sit in cafés with matcha and cake. It's hard to imagine a bigger contrast.

Durrës: antiquity, beach, street dogs

Durrës has a Roman amphitheatre right between apartment blocks, plus the columns of a Byzantine forum on a street corner. On the main square, street dogs doze in the sun while the red-and-black flag waves in front of a tower block. And in the evening on the beach a dog came by, sat down with us and just wanted to be stroked.

The abandoned villa on the hill

On a hill above the city stands an abandoned villa. Peeling plaster, empty window frames, a staircase leading nowhere. Out on the terrace a rusty frame – and behind it a view over all of Durrës.

Bunk'Art 1: the bunker on the edge of town

But my strongest moment in Tirana was Bunk'Art 1: a huge nuclear bunker on the edge of town, built for dictator Hoxha and his government - and almost empty when I was there. It impressed me more than many a big museum. My advice: take the bus out to Bunk'Art 1, then come back to the centre and spend the evening in Blloku.

What makes Albania special

Albania has mountains rising to around 2,700 metres, beaches on the Ionian Sea that rival Greece - and prices that make you look twice. By the way, a nod here often means "no" and a shake of the head "yes".

A little history

For more than 400 years Albania was part of the Ottoman Empire, and it became independent in 1912. After the Second World War Enver Hoxha sealed the country off almost completely until 1985 and had hundreds of thousands of bunkers built out of fear of invasion. In 1967 he declared Albania the world's first atheist state. Only in 1991 did the borders open.

My verdict

Albania is raw, warm and not yet polished. That's exactly why you should go now – before everyone else does.
