The Netherlands: more than just Amsterdam

Amsterdam, The Hague, Scheveningen, Nijmegen, Arnhem, Venlo: for me the Netherlands is the country of short distances - from NRW you are there in an hour, and every town feels different.

What makes the Netherlands special

There are more bicycles than inhabitants, and a large part of the country lies below sea level - protected by dykes, dunes and pumping stations. Add cheese markets with porters in white, fries with fritessaus and a laid-back attitude that is contagious.

A little history

In the 17th century, the Golden Age, the small republic was a world power: the East India Company, founded in 1602, is considered the world's first public company, and Rembrandt and Vermeer painted in this era. Amsterdam is the capital, but the country is governed from The Hague. Nijmegen is regarded as the oldest town in the country, and in 1944 one of the most famous battles of the war was fought over the bridge at Arnhem.

My tips town by town

Amsterdam: the Jordaan early, while the canals are still yours, and the free ferry to the NDSM wharf. The Hague: Vermeer's "Girl with a Pearl Earring" at the Mauritshuis in the morning, the tram to the beach at Scheveningen in the afternoon. Arnhem: free white bikes through Hoge Veluwe National Park. Venlo: market, coffee by the Maas and a portion of fries.

Crazy but true

Amsterdam has a houseboat just for cats and a bar where sailors supposedly paid with monkeys. And in Baarle the border with Belgium runs straight through living rooms and cafés. All on my map.
