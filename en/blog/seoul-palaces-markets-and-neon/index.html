Seoul: palaces, markets and neon

After Japan, Seoul was my next stop - and the contrast could hardly have been bigger. Where Tokyo is quiet, Seoul is loud, fast and hungry.

What makes South Korea special

A city of around ten million people where you can still take the subway to a granite peak in a national park. Food stalls where you eat mung bean pancakes on stools. And an alphabet so logical you can learn to read it in an afternoon.

A little history

Seoul has been a capital since 1394, then of the Joseon dynasty - Gyeongbokgung Palace dates from that era. King Sejong had the Hangul alphabet created in 1443 so that ordinary people could read too. After the Korean War the city lay in ruins; its rapid rise is still called the "Miracle on the Han River".

My tip for one day

In the morning stroll through Bukchon Hanok Village with its old wooden houses, at lunchtime head to the food stalls of Gwangjang Market and in the evening go up Namsan to N Seoul Tower as the city starts to glow.

Crazy but true

At the border with North Korea you can walk in a helmet into a tunnel that North Korea secretly dug under the border - discovered in 1978. And at Noryangjin Fish Market you pick your dinner alive from the tank. You will find both on my map.
