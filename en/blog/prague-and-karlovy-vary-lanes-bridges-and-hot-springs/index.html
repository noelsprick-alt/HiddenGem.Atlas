Prague & Karlovy Vary: lanes, bridges and hot springs

Everyone knows Prague - hardly anyone knows Karlovy Vary. I have been to both, and the best of Prague was not on Charles Bridge but one lane away from it.

What makes Czechia special

Hardly any country drinks as much beer per head as Czechia, and pilsner was invented here. Add to that an offbeat sense of humour that stands all over the city: the artist David Černý has filled Prague with sculptures - Sigmund Freud hanging from a pole above the street, a rotating Kafka head and babies crawling up the TV tower.

A little history

Under Emperor Charles IV Prague became one of the most important cities in Europe in the 14th century - construction of Charles Bridge began in 1357. Twice angry Praguers threw officials out of a window; the second defenestration in 1618 helped trigger the Thirty Years' War. In 1989 communism ended in the Velvet Revolution without a shot being fired. Karlovy Vary is also said to go back to Charles IV: legend has it he discovered the hot springs while hunting.

My experience

I was in Prague in 2024. I learnt what the real Prague is in Malá Strana: as soon as you turn off Charles Bridge into the side lanes it gets quiet - cobblestones, palace gardens, the tram squealing round the corner. My advice: cross the bridge at 7 am and head straight into the Lesser Town before the groups arrive.

In Karlovy Vary you walk from colonnade to colonnade with a drinking cup and taste the healing water. It does not taste good - but it is part of the experience. As a reward there is Becherovka afterwards, a liqueur whose recipe supposedly only two people know.

Crazy places you should not miss

The paternoster lift in the New City Hall that you simply hop into. The metronome in Letná Park, where the world's largest Stalin monument once stood. And an hour to the east, the Sedlec Ossuary with a chandelier made of human bones. You will find them all on my map.
