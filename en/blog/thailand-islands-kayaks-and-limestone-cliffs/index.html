Thailand: islands, kayaks and limestone cliffs

Almost three weeks in Thailand in February 2026: first Phuket, then Krabi. I was expecting beaches. What I got was Muay Thai, a cave you can only get through with a headlamp, monkeys as neighbours and a kayak trip between limestone cliffs.

Phuket: Buddha, old town, crickets by the spoon

The Big Buddha sits 45 metres tall in white marble above the island – from below it looks almost unreal. In Phuket Old Town I stumbled into a Chinese shrine between colourful Sino-Portuguese houses, all golden dragons and incense. And then, at the market: fried crickets and silkworm pupae, paid for by the spoon. Next to them durian, dragon fruit and longan, as if it were the most normal thing in the world. Here, it is.

Krabi: limestone wherever you look

In Krabi the landscape turns dramatic. Limestone cliffs rise out of the jungle and the sea everywhere. I kayaked between them and rode a scooter along country roads between palm trees and power lines. In Ao Nang a mother monkey sat by the roadside with her baby, as if waiting for the bus. And once a monitor lizard was simply lying in the middle of the path.

Muay Thai, hot springs and a cave

An evening at the Ao Nang boxing stadium: noise, sweat, referees, packed stands – the full programme, right in the middle of Ao Nang. During the day, the opposite: in the hot springs you sit in warm water in the middle of the forest with tree roots above you. And into one cave we went with headlamps only. From another, a steep staircase led out into green light.

What stays with me

A café on a hill, iced coffee in hand, nothing but jungle and karst mountains all the way to the horizon. A longtail boat at sunset. And a storm rolling grey and dramatic over the islands.

What makes Thailand special

Thailand is the only country in Southeast Asia never colonised by a European power - something people are still proud of. More than 90 per cent are Buddhists, and everywhere you see little spirit houses with offerings in front of homes and shops.

A little history

The history of the Thai state began with the Kingdom of Sukhothai in the 13th century, followed later by magnificent Ayutthaya. Until 1939 the country was called Siam. Krabi's karst cliffs are made of limestone that formed on the seabed hundreds of millions of years ago - which is why they rise so steeply out of the water today.

My verdict

Phuket to start, Krabi for the scenery. If you only lie on the beach, you miss the best part. Book kayaking, island hopping or a cooking class in advance – the good time slots go fast.
