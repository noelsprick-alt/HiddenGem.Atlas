5 things you didn't know about Düsseldorf

Düsseldorf is more than the Kö and the old town. I am there a lot - and these are five things that keep surprising me.

1. The cartwheelers

The city symbol is children doing cartwheels. Legend has it that children celebrated the victory at the Battle of Worringen in 1288 this way, after which Düsseldorf received its town charter. Cartwheel competitions are still held today, and you will find the little figures on many corners.

2. The Rhine Tower is a clock

The lights on the shaft of the Rhine Tower are not decoration - they show the time. The "Lichtzeitpegel" is considered the largest decimal clock in the world. Once you understand it, you will look at it every evening.

3. Little Tokyo

Around Immermannstraße lives one of the largest Japanese communities in Europe. Japanese bakeries, supermarkets, ramen - and in May, Japan Day with fireworks over the Rhine. I was there in 2025, and it feels like a short trip to Japan.

4. Altbier and the Köbes

In the old-town brewhouses you are served by the Köbes - a waiter with a blue apron and a loose tongue. He puts a fresh Altbier in front of you unasked as soon as your glass is empty. Only a beer mat on top of the glass ends the game.

5. Flingern - my favourite neighbourhood

My day to unwind: Saturday market at Hermannplatz, cake at Café Hüftgold, browsing at LANGBRETT in an old petrol station and the SKAND Klub in the evening. No sightseeing, just a neighbourhood that feels like home. You will find all the addresses on my map.
