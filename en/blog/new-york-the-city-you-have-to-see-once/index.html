New York: the city you have to see once

New York is the city from a thousand films - and still different when you are standing in it. I have been there. And the best part: many of the city's most impressive experiences cost nothing at all.

What makes New York special

In hardly any other city in the world are so many languages spoken. Every neighbourhood is its own little world - from Chinatown to Little Italy, from Wall Street to Williamsburg. And the subway runs around the clock.

A little history

Before it was New York it was called Nieuw Amsterdam - a Dutch trading settlement on Lenape land. In 1664 the English took over and renamed it. Between 1892 and 1954 some twelve million immigrants entered the US via Ellis Island - many American families can trace their history back to this island.

My tip

Walk across Brooklyn Bridge towards Manhattan early in the morning, then along the High Line and into Chelsea Market for lunch. In the evening choose Williamsburg over Times Square. And for the Statue of Liberty: the free Staten Island Ferry instead of an expensive boat tour.

Crazy but true

Grand Central has a whispering gallery where you can whisper to a friend across the room. Beneath City Hall lies a magnificent ghost station from 1904 that you can see from the 6 train. And you reach one speakeasy through a phone booth in a hot-dog shop. All on my map.
