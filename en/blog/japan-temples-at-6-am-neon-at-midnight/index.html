Japan: temples at 6 am, neon at midnight

Japan is a land of contrasts: alone beneath a thousand red gates at six in the morning, in the middle of Shibuya's neon at night. In 2022 I visited Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka - and what impressed me most was not the places but the people.

What makes Japan special

Politeness is no cliché here: people queue neatly, lost property comes back, trains run to the second. At the same time Japan is wonderfully crazy - temples full of beckoning cats, deer that bow to you and bars that barely fit six guests.

A little history

For more than a thousand years - from 794 to 1869 - Kyoto was the emperor's capital. From the 17th century Japan shut itself off almost completely from the rest of the world for around 200 years. Only with the Meiji Restoration of 1868 did the country open up and modernise radically within a few decades - which is why wooden temples and skyscrapers often stand right next to each other today.

My experience

At 6 am the gates of Fushimi Inari in Kyoto were almost all mine - for me the moment Kyoto made sense. And Shibuya at night is Tokyo in its purest form: neon, people, energy. What stays with me is something small: how people treat each other. Kindness costs nothing.

My tip

In Kyoto spend the first hours at Fushimi Inari, then Higashiyama while the lanes are quiet. In Tokyo do the Meiji Shrine and Harajuku by day and Shibuya at sunset. In Osaka drift through Dotonbori in the evening and eat takoyaki straight from the stall. On my map you will also find the temple of 1,200 stone figures and Drunkard's Alley.
